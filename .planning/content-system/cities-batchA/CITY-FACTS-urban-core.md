# Urban-Core Essex County Cities — Fact Bank (Cities Batch A)

House style = **name-only attribution** in prose (cite the body by name; no
outbound links / URLs in on-page copy). The four cities here share one NJ UCC
permit rule and one climate baseline — do NOT re-derive either; cross-reference
the shared packs. What differs city-to-city is the **construction office**, the
**historic COA gate** (only Newark and Orange have one), the **Census housing
profile**, the **verified neighborhoods**, and the **geography/flood stressors**.

### 1. CONSTRUCTION / PERMIT OFFICE per city (verified live)

- **Newark** — the office that administers construction permits and enforces the
  NJ Uniform Construction Code is the **Office of Uniform Construction Code (UCC)
  / Building Division within the City of Newark DEPARTMENT OF ENGINEERING**
  (Central Permit Office, **920 Broad Street, City Hall**). This is **NOT** the
  Department of Economic & Housing Development — that is a development/policy
  department, not the UCC enforcing agency. Use "Newark Department of Engineering
  — Building Division / Office of Uniform Construction Code." (Verified: City of
  Newark .gov, newarknj.gov/543 + Engineering department page.)
- **East Orange** — the **Building Division**, a designated State Uniform
  Construction Code Enforcement Agency, within the **Department of Property
  Maintenance** (East Orange City Hall, **44 City Hall Plaza**). Construction-permit
  applications are submitted there in person.
- **Orange (City of Orange Township)** — the **City of Orange Township Building &
  Construction Division**, housed under **Planning & Economic Development**;
  issues building/electrical/plumbing/fire permits, inspections, and certificates
  of occupancy.
- **Irvington** — the **Township of Irvington's construction-code (building)
  office** (the codified ordinance, Chapter 240, calls it the "Construction Code
  Department"; the township website presents "Building Construction"). Use the
  generic "Township of Irvington's construction-code (building) office"; do NOT
  name a specific director or fee on-page without live verification.
- **All four** apply the same statewide UCC rule (cross-ref
  facts-nj-regulatory-climate.md §1): a **detached one- or two-family dwelling
  reroof — including a full tear-off and replacement of the roof covering — is
  "ordinary maintenance" under N.J.A.C. 5:23-2.7 and requires NO construction
  permit, no inspection, no notice.** A permit IS required on commercial,
  multi-family, or attached buildings (the 25% rule) and for any structural roof
  work. Each city enforces but does not override the state classification.

### 2. HISTORIC COA GATE per city (CRITICAL — only assert a COA where a LOCAL designation is CONFIRMED)

- **Newark = YES.** The **Newark Landmarks & Historic Preservation Commission**
  issues Certificates of Appropriateness under **Newark Municipal Code Chapter
  41:10** (Historic Sites and Districts / Newark Landmarks and Historic
  Preservation Regulations). The ordinance auto-designates districts and landmarks
  that were on the National or State Register **as of May 30, 2007** — the
  ordinance's own words are **"shall automatically be designated a local
  landmark"** (use "local landmark"; the auto-designated list is **Exhibit A** on
  file with the City Clerk). **James Street Commons** (NR 1978) and **Lincoln
  Park** are confirmed locally designated. **Forest Hill** (NR 1990, ref #90001193),
  **Four Corners**, **Military Park Commons** (NR 2004), and **Weequahic Park**
  (NR 2003) are **INFERRED-local** from the pre-2007 auto-designation rule and are
  **NOT verified against the City-Clerk Exhibit A list** → HEDGE: do NOT state
  "this district is local-designated, COA applies" as flat fact; instead write
  "Newark's ordinance auto-designates pre-May-30-2007 Register districts as local
  landmarks — **verify a specific parcel's local/contributing status before
  asserting a COA.**"
- **Orange = YES.** The **City of Orange Township Historic Preservation
  Commission**, established by ordinance at **Code Ch. 210, Art. X (§§210-91 purpose
  / 210-92 designation criteria / 210-93 COA actions)** pursuant to N.J.S.A.
  40:55D-107 et seq., issues a **binding (decisional) COA** for regulated exterior
  work — including roofing — on properties in its **four locally designated
  districts: Orange Valley, Montrose/Seven Oaks Park, Main Street, and St. John's.**
  Orange is the only Batch A city besides Newark with a COA gate. Do NOT assert a
  COA for a parcel unless it falls inside one of those four designated districts.
- **East Orange = NO** confirmed local HPC or ordinance. The city's own **2006
  Master Plan Historic Preservation Element** documents that none exists and only
  **recommends creating one**. Its listed resources — the **Central Avenue
  Commercial Historic District**, **Brick Church** and **East Orange** stations,
  the **Ambrose-Ward Mansion**, and the **VA Medical Center** — are **National
  and/or NJ-Register-ONLY** → **NO COA.** Per the National Park Service, Register
  listing alone places no federal restriction on a private property owner. SHPO
  opinion-letter "eligible" sites (East Hill, Elmwood Avenue, the various
  streetscapes, Bakery Village, 75 Prospect Ave) are eligibility-track only — NOT
  designated, NOT COA-triggering.
- **Irvington = NO** local HPC, **no** local historic district, and **NO NRHP
  listings at all** in the township → **cut any COA step entirely.** Correct
  framing: "Irvington has no local historic-district ordinance, so a homeowner
  reroof faces no Certificate-of-Appropriateness step." (Verify against the
  current municipal code before publishing as an absolute.)

### 4. GEOGRAPHY corrections

- **Orange** does NOT contain or border the **South Mountain Reservation**. The
  Reservation is in West Orange/Maplewood/Millburn and borders South Orange;
  Orange borders **West Orange** to its west and is one municipality removed.
  Reframe Orange's tree/branch stressor to its **own mature street trees + the
  wooded West Orange / first-Watchung ridge to its west**, never to a Reservation
  adjacency.
- **Newark**: Newark Liberty International Airport (EWR) **straddles Newark AND
  Elizabeth** (Essex/Union county line) — do NOT call it "entirely in Newark." The
  **Passaic River** is Newark's **eastern/northeastern boundary**, draining to
  **Newark Bay** — do NOT conflate it with the Hackensack River (which is west of
  Newark Bay).
- **East Orange**: "Main Street" and "Dr. Martin Luther King Jr. Boulevard" are
  the **SAME corridor** (renamed 1987), not two streets. Only **Brick Church** and
  **East Orange** NJ Transit stations are active; **Ampere Station closed 1991 and
  was demolished**. The hospital is **CareWell Health Medical Center** (rebranded
  from East Orange General Hospital, Jan 2022) — use the current name. East Orange
  is a built-out inner-ring suburb on the flat Newark/Watsessing plain — NOT
  adjacent to a reservation, major river, or waterfront.
- **Irvington**: **Vailsburg is a NEWARK neighborhood** bordering Irvington's
  eastern edge (NOT an Irvington neighborhood). **Hilton is a section of
  MAPLEWOOD** across Irvington's western Springfield Avenue border (NOT Irvington).
  **I-78 only touches Irvington's southeastern border "very briefly" at Exit 54**
  — it does NOT bisect the township. **Olympic Park** (amusement park, 1887–1965,
  closed) survives only as the origin of the "Olympic Park" neighborhood NAME —
  present strictly as history.
