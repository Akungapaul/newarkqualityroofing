# West-Essex Essex County Cities — Fact Bank (Cities Batch C)

House style = **name-only attribution** in prose (cite the body by name; no outbound
links / URLs in on-page copy). The five cities here share one NJ UCC permit rule and
one climate baseline — do NOT re-derive either; cross-reference the shared packs. What
differs city-to-city is the **construction office**, the **historic COA gate** (which
varies sharply — read Rule 1 carefully), the **Census housing profile**, the **verified
neighborhoods**, and the **geography / reservation adjacency**.

These are the **west-essex archetype**: Watchung-ridge and valley towns on the western
side of Essex County, with a shared **mature street-tree canopy + reservation-edge**
roofing stressor framed QUALITATIVELY (leaf/debris clogging valleys and gutters,
branch-impact in nor'easters and summer storms, shade-driven moss/algae on north-facing
slopes). Four of the five (West Orange, Montclair, Verona, Cedar Grove) sit on or
between the Watchung ridges and contain/border a large Essex County reservation; Glen
Ridge is the exception — an inner LOWLAND borough whose defining stressor is mature
canopy, NOT ridge elevation. Owner-occupancy is HIGH across the archetype (these are
strongly homeowner-facing markets) — Glen Ridge (~93%) and West Orange (69.2%) are the
extremes, while Montclair runs a notably higher renter/multi-family share (56.9% owner).

### 1. HISTORIC COA GATE per city (CRITICAL — the binding gate is the LOCAL ordinance + COA, NOT Register listing, NOT a private deed covenant)

**The binding historic gate is the LOCAL ordinance + COA from the municipal HPC, NOT
National/State Register listing, and NOT a private deed covenant.** Per the National
Park Service, "Listing in the National Register places no federal restrictions on a
private property owner" — Register listing (or mere Register-ELIGIBILITY) alone never
restricts a private reroof. A private community covenant (e.g., Llewellyn Park's 1857
deed of trust / Committee of Managers) is likewise NOT a municipal COA. Only assert a
COA requirement where a LOCAL designation is CONFIRMED, and only for a parcel that falls
inside the designated district / on the designation list. This rule is the opposite of a
single flat rule across the five cities — do NOT flatten them.

- **West Orange = YES, but ONLY for locally designated landmarks.** The Township of
  West Orange HAS its own local Historic Preservation Commission (WOHPC) and ordinance —
  codified as **§25-30 "Historic Preservation Commission" within Chapter 25 (Land Use
  Regulations)** of the Revised General Ordinances (enacted/amended by Ord. 2357-12 and
  Ord. 2359-12; ordinance drafted c.1990, commission established/seated 1992). A
  **Certificate of Appropriateness from the WOHPC is required before the Construction
  Official issues a permit** for specified work (demolition, relocation, substantial
  exterior alteration) on a **LOCALLY designated landmark** — the WOHPC reports roughly
  **TEN locally designated township sites**. Correct framing: "If your property is one
  of West Orange's ~ten locally designated historic landmarks, exterior roofing work
  requires a Certificate of Appropriateness from the West Orange HPC before a
  construction permit issues." Do NOT assert COA review township-wide, and do NOT assert
  it for a National-Register-only or NR-eligible property.
  - **Llewellyn Park = PRIVATE COVENANT, NOT a municipal COA.** Llewellyn Park (founded
    1854/1857; America's first romantically landscaped gated planned community) is
    governed internally by a **private Committee of Managers under an 1857 deed of trust
    and private covenants** — NOT the West Orange HPC. It is ALSO National-Register-listed
    (Llewellyn Park Historic District, NRHP 1985, ~421.8 acres), and Register listing
    alone imposes no private restriction. Correct framing: "Llewellyn Park homeowners
    answer to the private Llewellyn Park community/covenant, not a township COA — **except
    for any individual structure the township has separately LOCALLY designated.**" A WOHPC
    source snippet lists a **"Llewellyn Park Gate House" among the township's locally
    designated landmarks**, so a specific structure inside the park may carry a genuine
    municipal COA. Do NOT over-correct into "nothing in Llewellyn Park is ever a municipal
    COA matter" — verify the Gate House (and any specific parcel) against the Township's
    current Landmark Designation Reports before publishing either way.
  - **Glenmont / Thomas Edison National Historical Park = FEDERAL, not a homeowner COA.**
    Glenmont (Edison's estate, inside Llewellyn Park) is federally administered by the
    National Park Service — destination/heritage context only, NOT a reroof-permitting
    matter.
- **Montclair = YES, conditional on a designated LOCAL district or local landmark.** The
  Township of Montclair has a strong, active local HPC under the **Historic Preservation
  Ordinance (Article XXIII of Chapter 347)** — HPC established at §§ 347-129 to 347-131;
  the COA requirement at **§ 347-136**; the LOCAL districts designated at **§ 347-148.1
  (Article XXIV)**. A **Certificate of Appropriateness is required for exterior changes
  that alter appearance** on (1) a Montclair **Local Historic Landmark** or (2) a property
  inside one of the **FOUR locally designated districts: Town Center, Upper Montclair
  (Business), Pine Street, and Watchung Plaza.** Correct framing: "If your property is a
  Montclair local landmark or is in a Montclair locally designated historic district,
  exterior roofing that changes its appearance requires HPC review / a Certificate of
  Appropriateness." ROOFING NUANCE: the township's own guidance EXEMPTS in-kind
  maintenance/repair with no change in design, scale, or appearance — do NOT overstate
  "every reroof in a local district needs a COA." Do NOT assert a COA for the **Estate
  Section / Estate Area** (nominated only, NOT codified in § 347-148.1), nor township-wide,
  nor for any Register-only district (Montclair has ~6 NR/State-Register districts and
  ~52 NR / ~100 SR listed sites — Register listing alone imposes no private restriction).
- **Glen Ridge = YES — BINDING LOCAL COA (the brief's "Register-only?" worry is REFUTED).**
  Glen Ridge adopted a local Historic Preservation Ordinance and established a Historic
  Preservation Commission **by municipal ordinance in 1987**, codified as **Chapter 15.32
  "Historic Preservation"** of the Borough Code (recently amended via Ordinance No. 1827,
  2025). The HPC issues **binding Certificates of Appropriateness** for exterior
  alterations — including roof replacement, change of roofing material, dormers, and
  visible roof-mounted equipment — on regulated/contributing properties in the **Glen
  Ridge Historic District, which covers over 90% of the borough**, so MOST homes are
  inside the COA-regulated district. A COA is valid two years from issuance. Frame the
  COA as a **LOCAL-ordinance matter (Ch. 15.32 / the HPC), NOT "because it's on the
  National Register"** — the 1982 NRHP listing alone imposes no private restriction;
  advise owners to confirm a specific parcel's status with the HPC/Building Department
  (boundary is on the official district map). Do NOT assert literally 100% of the borough
  is regulated.
- **Verona = YES, but ONLY for the two locally designated landmarks (and frame as "HPC
  review," not a named COA).** The Township of Verona has a real, active local Historic
  Preservation Commission (a/k/a Landmarks Preservation Commission) under **Zoning
  Ordinance Chapter 150, Article XXII "Historic Preservation," §§ 150-15.4 to 150-15.14**
  (authority under N.J.S.A. 40:55D-107 et seq.). Per the HPC's own materials, once a
  property is **locally designated**, "any proposed demolition, relocation, significant
  exterior architectural changes, new construction, subdivision or variances must be
  reviewed by the Historic Preservation Commission prior to the issuance of permits" —
  and in-kind exterior repairs are EXEMPT. Exactly **TWO locally designated landmarks**
  exist: the **Erie Railroad Freight Shed (62 Depot Street)** and the **Verona United
  Methodist Church (Montrose Avenue)**. Do NOT assert a binding "Certificate of
  Appropriateness" by that literal name — Verona's materials say "HPC review prior to
  issuance of permits." Do NOT claim the **Afterglow** section requires review (PROPOSED
  2017 survey recommendation only, NOT designated). Do NOT call **Verona Park** Register-
  listed (it is an Olmsted-designed Essex COUNTY park, absent from TCLF's NR-listed Essex
  parks list).
- **Cedar Grove = NO — no local HPC, no COA.** Cedar Grove has **NO local Historic
  Preservation Commission with COA power, NO historic-preservation ordinance/landmark
  chapter, and NO locally designated district or landmark.** A typical homeowner reroof
  needs **no Certificate of Appropriateness.** CORRECTION (do not over-state the absence):
  Cedar Grove DOES have a codified **Heritage Advisory Committee**, but it is
  **advisory/educational ONLY** (museum/cultural/genealogical/historic-research programs;
  NO designation, NO COA, NO regulatory authority). State it as "Cedar Grove has only an
  advisory Heritage Advisory Committee — no regulatory Historic Preservation Commission or
  COA process," NOT "nothing historic exists." A 2020 Master Plan Historical Preservation
  Plan element exists but is a planning document, not a regulatory gate. No NRHP/NJRHP
  listing was even found within Cedar Grove (Essex); the "Cedar Grove School" NRHP entry
  is in Madison Township, Middlesex County — a different place.

### 2. CONSTRUCTION / PERMIT OFFICE per city

- **All five** apply the same statewide UCC rule (cross-ref
  facts-nj-regulatory-climate.md §1): a **detached one- or two-family dwelling reroof —
  including a full tear-off and replacement of the roof covering — is "ordinary
  maintenance" under N.J.A.C. 5:23-2.7 and requires NO construction permit, no
  inspection, no notice.** A permit IS required on commercial, multi-family, or attached
  buildings (the 25% rule) and for any structural roof work; recover-vs-tear-off is
  governed by Rehab Subcode 5:23-6.4. Each city enforces but does not override the state
  classification. A COA in a local historic district is a **SEPARATE** approval — not a
  substitute for, nor a trigger of, a building permit; a 1–2 family reroof stays UCC
  "ordinary maintenance" even where a COA is required.
- **West Orange** — **Building & Construction Code Enforcement (the Building Department /
  Building & Construction Code Division), within the Department of Planning & Development**
  (66 Main Street, 2nd Floor). Administers/enforces the State UCC (N.J.A.C. 5:23) and
  issues all Building/Electrical/Plumbing/Fire construction permits; plan review for a
  complete application runs ~20 business days. Name the FUNCTION (Building & Construction
  Code Enforcement / the Construction Official) — do NOT print the named Construction
  Official (a 2026 listing showed "Tom Biondi, Jr.") or any phone/email/fee without live
  verification.
- **Montclair** — the **Township of Montclair Building Office (a/k/a Building Department)**,
  the local UCC enforcing agency via the Construction Code Official and
  building/plumbing/electrical/fire-protection subcode officials. Name the FUNCTION; do NOT
  publish a specific official's name, phone, or fee without live verification.
- **Glen Ridge** — the **Borough of Glen Ridge Building Department / Construction Code
  office**, supervised by a state-licensed Construction Official (Borough Hall, 825
  Bloomfield Avenue). An online permit portal exists (publicized ~2026). Name the FUNCTION;
  do NOT name an official or publish phone/hours/fee without live verification.
- **Verona** — the **Township of Verona Department of Building and Inspections / Building
  Department**, supervised by a state-licensed Construction Official + subcode officials,
  at the **Municipal Building, 600 Bloomfield Avenue, Verona NJ 07044** (NOT the Community
  Center). Name the FUNCTION; do NOT print the official (live verification resolves the
  current official as Thomas G. Jacobsen; "Len Mendola" is stale — both time-sensitive).
- **Cedar Grove** — the **Township of Cedar Grove Building Department** (525 Pompton
  Avenue), which administers construction permits and inspections and enforces the NJ UCC.
  Name the FUNCTION (Building Department / Construction Official); do NOT name the
  individual official on-page (a listing showed "James Hoffmann" — time-sensitive).

### 3. RESERVATION GEOGRAPHY corrected (verified — do NOT misattribute)

- **South Mountain Reservation (~2,110 ac)** = located in portions of **Maplewood,
  Millburn, and WEST ORANGE; borders South Orange.** Among the five west-essex cities,
  **ONLY West Orange** contains/borders it — do NOT attribute it to Montclair, Verona,
  Cedar Grove, or Glen Ridge.
- **Eagle Rock Reservation (~408.33 ac, First Watchung Mountain)** = located in **West
  Orange, Montclair, AND Verona** (the Eagle Rock overlook, ~618 ft a.s.l. — a point
  elevation, not a city relief figure — marks the Montclair/West Orange boundary, in West
  Orange).
- **Mills Reservation (~157.15 ac)** = located in **Cedar Grove and Montclair.**
- **Hilltop Reservation (~284.16 ac, Second Watchung Mountain)** = located in **Cedar
  Grove, North Caldwell, and Verona.**
- **Glen Ridge borders NONE** of the large Essex County reservations (it is an inner
  lowland borough). **Brookdale Park** = Bloomfield/Montclair; **Glenfield Park** =
  Montclair (small corner into Glen Ridge) — neither is IN Glen Ridge.
- Glen Ridge has **ONE NJ Transit station (Glen Ridge, Montclair-Boonton Line)** — Bay
  Street and Watchung Avenue stations are in **Montclair**, do NOT attribute them to Glen
  Ridge.
