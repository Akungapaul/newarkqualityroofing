# FACT PACK — Green / Vegetated Roof vs Traditional (Membrane) Roof (NJ)

Definitions used throughout (per NJ SWBMP Manual Ch 9.4 + GSA): **Extensive** green roof = growing-medium layer **6 inches or less** (shallow sedum/groundcover, lightweight). **Intensive** green roof = growing-medium layer **6 inches or greater** (garden, shrubs, trees; heavy, often not retrofit-suitable). **Semi-intensive** = between the two.

## 1. STORMWATER RETENTION / DETENTION

| Claim | Value | Source | Tier |
|---|---|---|---|
| Green roofs reduce site peak flow rate | by **up to 65%**, and delay flow off-site by **up to ~3 hours** (depends on roof size/slope/medium) | GSA, "The Benefits and Challenges of Green Roofs on Public and Commercial Buildings" (2011) — exact text: "reducing peak flow rates by up to 65%" | [PRIMARY] |
| Extensive sedum green roof — annual rainfall retained | **~50–60%** for a 3.5–4″ deep sedum roof (central PA / NE US humid climate) | Penn State Green Roof Research Center (Dept. of Plant Science) — Stormwater Quantity | [SECONDARY-named] |
| Seasonal swing (extensive) | up to **~100%** retained May–Sep (warm season); **~20–30%** in cool/dormant months | Penn State Green Roof Research Center | [SECONDARY-named] |
| Extensive roofs — annual retention range across studies | **~34–69%**, average **~56%** | EPA "Soak Up the Rain: Green Roofs" / EPA Stormwater BMP fact sheet (summarizing peer literature) | [SECONDARY-named] |
| Intensive (deeper) roofs — annual retention | higher than extensive, commonly **~65–85%** of annual precipitation; deeper medium = more storage | EPA Stormwater BMP literature summary; corroborated by GSA (deeper roofs store more) | [SECONDARY-named] |
| Performance is rainfall-pattern dependent | % retention falls as storm size rises (saturated medium passes water); large storms still detain/slow runoff but retain less | GSA (2011); EPA BMP fact sheet | [PRIMARY-attrib] |
| Minimum thickness for meaningful CSO benefit | green roof **≥3 inches** on sufficient area can reduce summer combined-sewer-overflow frequency | GSA (2011) | [PRIMARY] |

**Writer guidance:** A defensible, safe headline for an Essex County page: *"Extensive (sedum) green roofs retain roughly 50–60% of annual rainfall and cut peak runoff by up to 65%, per the GSA green-roof study and Penn State green-roof research; intensive (garden-depth) roofs retain more because the deeper soil stores more water."* Do not state a single universal % — always frame as a range tied to roof depth and storm size.

## 2. DEAD LOADS — SATURATED (lb/sq ft)

| Claim | Value | Source | Tier |
|---|---|---|---|
| Test method for green-roof dead loads | **ASTM E2397** ("Determination of Dead Loads and Live Loads Associated with Vegetative (Green) Roof Systems"); paired with **ASTM E2399** (max media density for dead-load analysis) | ASTM International, E2397 / E2399 — both explicitly cited in NJ SWBMP Manual Ch 9.4 references | [PRIMARY] |
| GSA-measured wet weight, **3″ extensive** (Type II) | **20.06 lb/ft²** total system dead load (per-layer: 3″ growth medium 16.45 + plants 2.0 + fabrics/blanket ≈1.6) | GSA (2011), Table A3 "Wet weight load of green roof layers," measured per ASTM E2397 | [PRIMARY-attrib] |
| GSA-measured wet weight, **6″ semi-intensive** (Type III) | **42.23 lb/ft²** total (4″ growth media 26.41 + 2″ drainage media 10.97 + plants 3.0 + fabrics/blanket) | GSA (2011), Table A3, measured per ASTM E2397 | [PRIMARY-attrib] |
| General planning range — **extensive** saturated | commonly cited **~15–30 lb/ft²** (typ. 10–25 dry; +30–50% saturated) | Cost-aggregator / trade-summary range (EcoRoof Systems, Archtoolbox, Delaware DNREC GI fact sheet) — corroborates GSA's 20 lb/ft² | [SECONDARY] |
| General planning range — **intensive** saturated | commonly cited **~80–150 lb/ft² or more** | Trade/agency summaries (Archtoolbox, Delaware DNREC GI fact sheet) | [SECONDARY] |
| Roof slope limit (NJ) | **maximum 20%** roof slope for a green-roof BMP | NJ Stormwater BMP Manual, Ch 9.4 (Jan 2026), Stormwater Runoff Quantity criteria | [PRIMARY] |
| Structural verification is mandatory | Green roof adds dead load (assembly) + live loads (rainfall, people, equipment); "the structural load capacity of the roof is a crucial consideration" and "it is important to analyze the structural integrity of the roof before deciding whether to use a green roof" | NJ Stormwater BMP Manual, Ch 9.4 — "Structural Strength" + Introduction | [PRIMARY] |

**Writer guidance / code note:** Always state that a **NJ-licensed professional/structural engineer must verify the roof's load capacity** before any green roof — this is both a code/structural reality (IBC §1607.12.3 imposes the vegetative-roof load; NJ UCC adopts IBC) and is explicitly flagged by the NJ BMP Manual. Use GSA's measured numbers (20 / 42 lb/ft²) as the most defensible primary-tier figures; present the 15–30 / 80–150 ranges only as "general planning ranges." Heavier intensive loads are why intensive roofs "may not be suitable for retrofit projects" (NJ BMP Manual).

## 3. MEMBRANE PROTECTION / LONGEVITY

| Claim | Value | Source | Tier |
|---|---|---|---|
| Green roof extends membrane life | "Properly installed green roofs **more than double** the number of years before a roof needs to be replaced, as compared with conventional and white counterparts" | GSA (2011), §2.10 Roof Longevity (verbatim) | [PRIMARY-attrib] |
| Mechanism | vegetation + growing medium shield the membrane from **UV radiation** and **temperature-extreme fluctuations** (daily expansion/contraction) that wear membranes out | GSA (2011) §2.10; echoed by NJ SWBMP Manual Ch 9.4 ("protect the roof and the waterproofing layer from physical damage and ultra-violet radiation") | [PRIMARY-attrib] |
| GSA modeling life expectancy | green roof **40 years** vs conventional/black roof **17 years** (used as the cost-benefit-model assumption) | GSA (2011), §2.10.2 and Executive Summary | [PRIMARY-attrib] |
| Range across cited studies (GSA Table 7) | green vs black, in years: GRHC **25/17**; LBNL **29/14**; Fraunhofer Institute **40/15**; European Fed. of Green Roof Assns **60/30**; Mann (2002) **50/25**; AOC Dirksen study **50/17** | GSA (2011), Table 7 "Green roof membrane lifetime versus conventional roof membrane lifetime" | [PRIMARY-attrib] |
| Longest real-world durability anecdote | green roofs on several NCR federal buildings "have not been replaced since their installation in the **1930s**"; some Berlin green roofs ~100 yrs without failure | GSA (2011) §2.10.2 | [PRIMARY-attrib] |
| InterNACHI life-expectancy chart | **vegetated / green roof: 5–40 years** | InterNACHI, "Standard Estimated Life Expectancy Chart for Homes" | [SECONDARY-named] |

**Writer guidance / honest-positioning flag:** The "doubles membrane life / 40–60 yr" claim is **industry- and research-attributed, NOT a controlled-proven universal constant** — GSA itself notes "there is very little data regarding replacement of green roofs" and the life figures vary widely by study (25 to 60 yrs in Table 7). Always attribute: *"per the GSA green-roof study, covering the membrane can more than double its service life (GSA's model uses 40 vs 17 years)."* The InterNACHI **5–40 yr** range corroborates the upper end while honestly showing the **low end** — a green roof's membrane only lasts long if it is well built and well maintained; the bottom of InterNACHI's range reflects poor installs / leaks. NJ BMP Manual stresses "the waterproofing layer... is especially important, as failures can create serious structural problems," and that a leak under a green roof is hard to locate (a leak-detection method must be in the maintenance plan). Do NOT present 40–60 yrs as guaranteed.

## 4. ENERGY / URBAN HEAT ISLAND

| Claim | Value | Source | Tier |
|---|---|---|---|
| Green-roof surface temperature reduction | green-roof surface can be **up to 56°F lower** than a conventional roof surface | EPA, "Using Green Roofs to Reduce Heat Islands" | [PRIMARY] |
| Ambient air-temperature effect | green roofs can reduce nearby air temperature by **up to 20°F** | EPA, "Using Green Roofs to Reduce Heat Islands" | [PRIMARY] |
| Building cooling-load / indoor-temp effect | can reduce cooling load by **up to 70%** and lower indoor air temperature by **up to 27°F** vs conventional roof (study-dependent, building-specific) | EPA, "Using Green Roofs to Reduce Heat Islands" | [PRIMARY] |
| Mechanism — distinct from reflective roofs | green roofs cool primarily via **evapotranspiration** (vegetation moving/evaporating water) + shading + insulation — this is different from a cool/reflective roof, which works by high **solar reflectance (albedo)** | EPA heat-island program; DOE / Cool Roof Rating Council (CRRC) reflectance framing | [PRIMARY-attrib] |
| Insulation effect | growing medium + vegetation act as added roof insulation, reducing heating and cooling energy | DOE Federal Technology Alert "Green Roofs"; EPA | [SECONDARY-named] |

**Writer guidance:** Use the EPA temperature numbers (56°F surface / 20°F air) as the headline — they are primary and durable. The 70% cooling-load / 27°F indoor figures are EPA-cited but **study-specific and building-dependent** — frame as "up to" and attribute to EPA; do NOT generalize to a Newark home. **Do NOT invent $/year savings.** Keep the cool-roof vs green-roof distinction clear: a white/reflective membrane cools by reflecting sun (high albedo, per CRRC/DOE); a green roof cools by evapotranspiration + insulation (per EPA). Both reduce heat-island effect by different physics.

## 6. NJ / ESSEX COUNTY STORMWATER + CODE REGULATORY CONTEXT

| Claim | Value | Source | Tier |
|---|---|---|---|
| Governing NJ stormwater rule | **N.J.A.C. 7:8** Stormwater Management Rules (NJDEP) | NJDEP, N.J.A.C. 7:8 | [PRIMARY] |
| Green infrastructure now required | 2020 amendments (effective **March 2, 2021**) require **major developments** to meet groundwater-recharge, runoff-quality, and runoff-quantity standards by **incorporating green infrastructure** (per N.J.A.C. 7:8-5.2(a)(2) → 5.3) | NJDEP N.J.A.C. 7:8; NJ Stormwater BMP Manual Ch 9.4 | [PRIMARY] |
| Green roof IS an accepted NJ Green Infrastructure BMP | Yes — listed in NJ SWBMP Manual Ch 9.4 as a Green Infrastructure BMP | NJ Stormwater BMP Manual (Jan 2026), Ch 9.4 | [PRIMARY] |
| ⚠️ What a NJ green roof is credited for | **Green Infrastructure: Yes** · **Stormwater Runoff Quantity: Yes** · **Groundwater Recharge: NOT ALLOWED** · **Stormwater Runoff Quality: NOT ALLOWED** | NJ Stormwater BMP Manual Ch 9.4, "Applicable Design and Performance Standards" table (verbatim) | [PRIMARY] |
| Quantity-credit mechanism | volume reduction via a **reduced Curve Number** based on the growing medium's available water capacity; max slope **20%**; min vegetation density **85%** | NJ Stormwater BMP Manual Ch 9.4 | [PRIMARY] |
| NJ design standards reference FLL + ASTM | growing-medium water retention tested by **ASTM E2399** or the **FLL Guidelines** method (≥35% by vol. extensive / ≥45% intensive, ≤65% either); permeability **ASTM E2396**; dead loads **ASTM E2397** | NJ Stormwater BMP Manual Ch 9.4 (cites FLL, ASTM E2396/E2397/E2399, ASTM E2777 guide) | [PRIMARY] |
| FLL Guidelines | Forschungsgesellschaft Landschaftsentwicklung Landschaftsbau (FLL, Germany) "Green Roof Guidelines" — the international reference standard for green-roof media/design; named in the NJ manual | FLL (named in NJ SWBMP Manual Ch 9.4) | [SECONDARY-named] |
| Maintenance plan + deed notice | a NJ green roof **must have a maintenance plan** and **must be reflected in a deed notice recorded in the county clerk's office** to prevent alteration/removal; leak-detection method required in the plan | NJ Stormwater BMP Manual Ch 9.4 | [PRIMARY] |
| Structural / load code basis | vegetative-roof dead+live loads governed by **IBC §1607.12.3 / 1607.12.3.1**; structural fire resistance **IBC §1507.16.1**; NJ adopts IBC via the **NJ Uniform Construction Code (NJ UCC)** | International Building Code (IBC); NJ UCC adoption | [PRIMARY-attrib] |
| Fire / setback requirement | IBC **§1505.10** references **ANSI/SPRI VF-1** ("External Fire Design Standard for Vegetative Roofs") for external fire design; VF-1 requires a **6-ft-wide Class A fire-rated (vegetation-free) zone** at intervals (e.g., ~every 125 linear ft / ~15,625 sq ft) and at certain perimeters/penetrations — a **Class A fire-rated membrane** can satisfy the fire-break requirement | International Building Code §1505.10; ANSI/SPRI VF-1 (named) | [PRIMARY-attrib] |
| Design wind speed (northern NJ / Essex) | basic design wind speed for the region is roughly **~110–115 mph** (Risk Category II) per ASCE 7; rooftop wind uplift on the green-roof assembly must be addressed (ANSI/SPRI **RP-14** / wind-design + ballast/blanket) | ASCE 7 (7-16 / 7-22) wind maps; ANSI/SPRI RP-14 (wind design) — *verify exact mph for the specific site via the ASCE 7 Hazard Tool* | [SECONDARY-named] |
| Newark / Essex local context | Newark is a dense, older urban area with **combined sewer** infrastructure (CSO concern) — exactly the GSA-described case where green roofs help most; municipal stormwater control ordinances must conform to N.J.A.C. 7:8 | GSA (2011) on CSO benefit; N.J.A.C. 7:8 municipal-ordinance requirement | [PRIMARY-attrib] |

**Writer guidance (NJ-critical):** The single most important NJ-specific fact for honest positioning: **in New Jersey a green roof earns stormwater RUNOFF-QUANTITY credit but is NOT allowed for groundwater-recharge or runoff-quality credit** under the state BMP Manual. Never imply a Newark green roof "recharges groundwater" or "treats runoff for water quality" for code purposes. Frame green roofs as a quantity/peak-flow + CSO-relief tool well suited to dense, combined-sewer Newark — and always note the mandatory **NJ-licensed structural engineer review**, **maintenance plan + recorded deed notice**, and **fire-break/Class-A** requirements.
