## directAnswer (35 words)
A green roof beats traditional membrane roofing on stormwater and heat — a green roof retains ~50–60% of rainfall and runs up to 56°F cooler (per the EPA) — while membrane roofing wins on cost and weight.

## intro
1. Defines BOTH entities by function + differentiator (R38): green roof = vegetated assembly (medium, plants, drainage, membrane) that retains rainfall and shields the membrane; traditional membrane roofing = exposed single-/multi-ply low-slope system (EPDM, TPO, modified bitumen, BUR) that sheds all rain.
2. Sub-types + failure modes per side (named-sourced): extensive (≤6in) vs intensive (≥6in) per NJ SWBMP Manual Ch 9.4, failure = hard-to-locate hidden membrane leak; membrane splits EPDM/TPO/mod-bit/BUR, failure modes seam separation / welded-seam failure / blistering-alligator cracking per InterNACHI + NRCA.

## verdict
- **winner:** green roof wins on stormwater + cooling; membrane wins on cost, weight, simplicity.
- **reasoning:** green roof over membrane when stormwater quantity drives — retains ~50–60%, cuts peak up to 65%, earns NJ runoff-quantity credit (BMP Ch 9.4).
- **alternateScenario:** membrane wins on upfront budget + load — $5–$10/sq ft at 1–5 lb/sq ft vs green's $10–$35 + ~20–150 lb/sq ft (HomeAdvisor, GSA).
