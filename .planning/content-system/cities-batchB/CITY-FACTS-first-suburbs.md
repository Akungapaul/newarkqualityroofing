# First-Suburbs Essex County Cities — Fact Bank (Cities Batch B)

House style = **name-only attribution** in prose (cite the body by name; no outbound
links / URLs in on-page copy). The five cities here share one NJ UCC permit rule and
one climate baseline — do NOT re-derive either; cross-reference the shared packs. What
differs city-to-city is the **construction office**, the **historic COA gate** (which
varies sharply — read Rule 2 carefully), the **Census housing profile**, the
**verified neighborhoods**, and the **geography/flood stressors**.

These are the **first-suburbs archetype**: older streetcar/railroad inner-ring towns,
predominantly pre-WWII to mid-century stock, with a shared **mature street-tree canopy**
roofing stressor framed QUALITATIVELY (leaf/debris clogging valleys and gutters,
branch-impact in nor'easters and summer storms, shade-driven moss/algae on north-facing
slopes). Owner-occupancy is HIGHER than the urban-core Batch A cities (these are
homeowner-facing markets) — except Bloomfield and Belleville, which run a notably higher
renter/multi-family share for a suburb.

### 1. CONSTRUCTION / PERMIT OFFICE per city

- **All five** apply the same statewide UCC rule (cross-ref
  facts-nj-regulatory-climate.md §1): a **detached one- or two-family dwelling reroof —
  including a full tear-off and replacement of the roof covering — is "ordinary
  maintenance" under N.J.A.C. 5:23-2.7 and requires NO construction permit, no
  inspection, no notice.** A permit IS required on commercial, multi-family, or attached
  buildings (the 25% rule) and for any structural roof work. Each city enforces but does
  not override the state classification.
- **Bloomfield** — construction permits / UCC enforcement are administered by the
  **Township of Bloomfield's construction/code office** (township pages use both
  "Construction Department" and "Community Development Department"; the township code
  establishes a "Construction Official"). Name the **function** (construction permits /
  UCC enforcement under the Construction Official), not a single fixed department title.
  Do NOT print the named Construction Official (time-sensitive personnel) on-page without
  live verification.
- **Belleville** — the **Township of Belleville Building & Construction Code office**
  (Department of Planning & Development), Belleville Town Hall, 3rd Floor, 152 Washington
  Avenue. Construction Official + building/plumbing/electrical/fire-protection subcode
  officials; the township has also adopted the International Property Maintenance Code.
- **Nutley** — the **Township of Nutley Code Enforcement Department** (Town Hall, 2nd

- **Maplewood** — the **Township of Maplewood Construction Division / Building
  Department** (Construction Official + subcode officials), within the Department of

### 2. HISTORIC COA GATE per city (CRITICAL — only assert a COA where a LOCAL designation is CONFIRMED)

**The binding historic gate is the LOCAL ordinance + COA from the municipal HPC, NOT
National/State Register listing.** Per the National Park Service, "Listing in the National
Register places no federal restrictions on a private property owner" — Register listing
alone never restricts a private reroof. Only assert a COA requirement where a LOCAL
designation is CONFIRMED, and only for a parcel that falls inside the designated district
/ on the designation list. This rule is the opposite of a single flat rule across the five
cities — do NOT flatten them.

- **Bloomfield = YES (CORRECTED — the prior "Register-only / no local regulation"
  assumption was WRONG).** The Township of Bloomfield HAS a local Historic Preservation
  Commission governed by **Chapter 302 "Historic Districts"** (Ord. No. 23-11, adopted
  March 27, 2023, superseding Ord. 14-32). Properties in the locally designated historic
  district require a **Historic Preservation Commission Application for any exterior
  project on the building/home or site work BEFORE a construction permit issues** — a
  Certificate-of-Appropriateness-type local review, and roof replacement (an exterior
  alteration) falls within it. **Local COA jurisdiction is set by the township's published
  "Historic District Property List"** — owners must check that list (or the HPC Secretary)
  to confirm a parcel. Do NOT assert a whole neighborhood (e.g., "all of Bloomfield
  Center") is regulated. Correct framing: "if your property is on Bloomfield's Historic
  District Property List, exterior roofing work requires an HPC application." Do NOT
  conflate the National Register **Bloomfield Green Historic District** (NR 1978) boundary
  with the LOCAL regulated district — they overlap around the Green/Center but are not
  necessarily identical, and NR listing alone imposes no restriction.
- **Nutley = YES.** The Township of Nutley Historic Preservation Committee, under the
  **Historic Preservation ordinance Chapter 410** (adopted 2-7-2012, Ord. 3193; amended
  in entirety 8-15-2017, Ord. 3361; authorized under N.J.S.A. 40:55D-107 et seq.), reviews
  exterior work and issues/denies **binding Certificates of Appropriateness** for the
  locally designated **"Historic District of the Third River and Environs."** CRITICAL:
  it is **Chapter 410, NOT "Chapter 272"** (Ch. 272 is the separate Construction Codes,
  Uniform chapter). A prior research pass FABRICATED "Ch. 272," a "$150 COA fee," a
  "$2,000/day fine," a "200-foot buffer zone," and a five-landmark list (Nutley Public
  Library / First Presbyterian Church / Historical Society Museum / Woodland Cemetery
  Chapel / former Town Hall) — **NONE are confirmed; do NOT publish any of them.** The
  **Enclosure** (NR-listed Dec 31, 1974) is **very likely WITHIN** the Third River district
  but is NOT named by parcel in the public code; do NOT flatly assert "a reroof in The
  Enclosure requires a COA" — write "verify the specific parcel against the Township's
  official historic-district map before asserting a COA." The district boundary map is on
  file with the Township, not in the public code.
- **South Orange = YES.** The Township of South Orange Village Historic Preservation
  Commission operates under **Village Code Chapter 185** and issues Certificates of
  Appropriateness for designated buildings/properties and properties in designated
  districts. The **Montrose Park Historic District** (~550 homes) is BOTH a **LOCALLY
  designated COA district** (COA required for exterior alterations including roofing, via
  the HPC under Ch. 185; COA checklist cites Village Ordinance #2012-09, current framework
  Ordinance 2024-16) AND **National/State Register-listed (NR listed Aug 29, 1997)**.
  Assert the COA as a **LOCAL-ordinance matter, NOT "because it's on the National
  Register,"** and only inside the designated district / for designated local landmarks —
  NOT Village-wide. The Township also designates individual Local Landmarks (Master Plan
  HP Element; recent Ch. 185 amendment activity); a complete inventory was not retrieved,
  so frame as "Montrose Park plus individually designated local landmarks — verify each."
  Other South Orange NR listings (Old Main DL&W Railroad Historic District, Prospect Street)
  are Register-only unless local designation is separately confirmed.
- **Maplewood = FRAMEWORK ONLY — do NOT assert an active local COA district.** Maplewood
  HAS a Historic Preservation Commission and a historic-preservation ordinance (the
  "Historic Preservation Ordinance of the Township of Maplewood," **Article VIII** of the
  municipal code) — so the COA mechanism EXISTS for locally designated properties. BUT no
  currently named, LOCALLY designated Maplewood historic district or landmark (COA-active)
  was confirmed. The prominent **Maplewood Village Historic District is NATIONAL/STATE
  REGISTER-ONLY** (listed 4/25/2022) — per The Village Green NJ and NPS, the listing
  "carries no additional regulation for private owners… who can renovate or demolish their
  buildings at will." **Cut any implication that a reroof in Maplewood Village requires a
  township COA.** Correct framing: "IF a property is in (or becomes) a LOCALLY designated
  Maplewood historic district or landmark, exterior roofing work can require a township COA
  — confirm current local designation with the Township before relying on it."
- **Belleville = HPC EXISTS, but do NOT assert a COA for a typical homeowner reroof.**
  The Belleville Historic Preservation Commission is an active municipal body (meets 3rd
  Thursday monthly), and it designated the **Old Reformed Church / Reformed Dutch Church
  of Second River** (171 Main Street) as a **LOCAL landmark on July 4, 2014** — the only
  confirmed local designation. The **COA-issuing scope** of Belleville's local ordinance
  was NOT confirmed, and no locally designated historic DISTRICT exists. For all other
  properties, frame historic context as **Register-only / honorary with NO private-reroof
  restriction.** A COA, if applicable at all, would apply only to that one 2014-designated
  landmark. The same church is also NR/NJ-Register-listed (NR Dec 21, 1978) — Register
  listing alone imposes no restriction (NPS).

### 4. GEOGRAPHY corrections (rivers + reservation adjacency)

- **Bloomfield — three distinct watercourses, keep them separate.** The **Third River**
  runs through Bloomfield near the **town center** (a USGS gage, "Third River at Bloomfield,"
  monitors it). **Watsessing Park's watercourses are the SECOND River + Toney's Brook —
  NOT the Third River.** **Cut any statement that the Third River runs through Watsessing
  Park.**
- **Belleville — rivers.** The **SECOND River** (not the Passaic) forms Belleville's border
  with **Newark**. The **Passaic River** is Belleville's **eastern/northeastern boundary**,
  with Belleville on the **WEST bank** (opposite North Arlington / Lyndhurst / Kearny). Do
  NOT write "Passaic River separates Belleville from Newark."
- **Nutley — rivers.** The **Third River (a/k/a Yantacaw)** runs THROUGH Nutley (through
  Yantacaw Park and Memorial Park). The **Passaic River BORDERS** the township (the western
  edge per Wikipedia). Do NOT swap them. The former Roche campus **ON3 STRADDLES Nutley AND
  Clifton** — do NOT describe it as entirely in Nutley.
- **Maplewood / South Orange — South Mountain Reservation.** The ~2,100-acre Reservation is
  **LOCATED IN portions of Maplewood, Millburn, and West Orange and BORDERS South Orange.**
  So **Maplewood partially CONTAINS it** (on Maplewood's WEST / NORTHWEST edge — NOT the
  east side); **South Orange only BORDERS it** (eastern border of the Reservation). Neither
  WHOLLY contains it. Keep the **Montrose Park Historic District in SOUTH ORANGE** (not
  Maplewood); Maplewood's NR district is **Maplewood Village** — do NOT mix them up.
