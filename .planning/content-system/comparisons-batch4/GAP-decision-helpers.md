## 2. Commercial No-Dollar-Limit (NDL) warranty — NEW (named sources; KEEP outright unless flagged)

The commercial/low-slope side is the genuine gap the existing packs don't cover. All figures below carry their named source inline; attribute in-text exactly as written.

### 2.1 What NDL means (KEEP)
- **A No-Dollar-Limit (NDL) manufacturer guarantee covers BOTH labor and material to repair covered leaks with no monetary cap on the repair obligation.** GAF's EverGuard Diamond Pledge NDL guarantee states GAF will repair covered leaks "resulting from a manufacturing defect, ordinary wear and tear, or workmanship in applying the GAF Roofing Materials," and that "There is no dollar limit on covered repairs." — Source: **GAF (EverGuard Diamond Pledge NDL Roof Guarantee, doc COMTS700).**
- **NDL removes the monetary cap that otherwise applies.** Johns Manville: a guarantee obligation "is limited to a specific number of years and, except in the case of No Dollar Limit Guarantees, a specified maximum monetary amount," and "the total expenditure per square for repairs varies from the Original Installed Cost (OIC) to NO DOLLAR LIMIT (NDL)." The non-NDL alternative caps payout at the **original installed cost (OIC)**; NDL removes that cap. — Source: **Johns Manville (Peak Advantage Guarantees, RS-7377).**
- **NDL does not prorate/depreciate with roof age** for the term. GAF's commercial comparison shows the base material warranty provides "replacement material… prorated based on use," while the Diamond Pledge NDL row provides "Yes, 100%" replacement material and the only "Yes" under "Unlimited dollar amount for covered repairs." — Source: **GAF (Commercial Roof Warranty and Guarantee Comparison Guide).**

### 2.2 The three commercial tiers (KEEP — use this as the page's commercial structure spine)
- **Material-only (base):** covers only defective materials, **prorated**, **no labor, no workmanship**, does not cover the entire system; per GAF's matrix it can be offered by "Anyone." — Source: **GAF (Commercial Roof Warranty and Guarantee Comparison Guide).**
- **System (middle tier):** covers the entire manufacturer system — **100% material + labor + workmanship** — but **still carries a dollar limit** on covered repairs (certified-contractor-only). — Source: **GAF (Commercial Roof Warranty and Guarantee Comparison Guide).**
- **NDL (top tier):** the system coverage above **with the dollar cap removed** — the only tier answering "Yes" to "Unlimited dollar amount for covered repairs." — Source: **GAF (Commercial Roof Warranty and Guarantee Comparison Guide).**

### 2.3 Edge-to-edge / whole-system coverage (KEEP)
- **NDL/system guarantees cover the whole installed system "edge-to-edge," not just the membrane sheet.** GAF lists repair of leaks "through the GAF roofing membrane, liquid-applied membrane or coating, base flashing, high-wall waterproofing flashing, insulation, expansion joint covers, preflashed accessories, and metal flashings"; Johns Manville describes its NDL coverage as "THE WHOLE SYSTEM." — Source: **GAF (Diamond Pledge NDL guarantee, COMTS700) and Johns Manville (Peak Advantage RS-7377).**

### 2.4 How an NDL guarantee is issued and kept valid (KEEP — high decision value)
- **An NDL guarantee is issued by the MANUFACTURER, not the contractor, only after an authorized install + manufacturer inspection.** Johns Manville: issued only "once a JM Roofing System has been installed by a JM Approved Roofing Contractor and is inspected and approved by a JM representative," and "NO DOLLAR LIMIT (NDL) GUARANTEES ARE ONLY AVAILABLE THROUGH NDL DESIGNATED CONTRACTORS." — Source: **Johns Manville (Peak Advantage Guarantees, RS-7377).**
- **The manufacturer requires advance notice/registration so it can inspect.** The JM Approved Roofing Contractor "must notify JM in writing at least two weeks before starting any roof that requires a Peak Advantage Guarantee," with a final manufacturer inspection on completion. — Figure: **minimum 14 days' advance notice + final manufacturer inspection.** Source: **Johns Manville (Peak Advantage Guarantees, RS-7377).**
- **Ongoing inspections + maintenance + recordkeeping are required to keep it valid.** GAF's Diamond Pledge NDL, under Owner Responsibilities: "You must perform regular inspections and maintenance and keep records of this work," and it excludes leaks caused by "inadequate roof maintenance, that is, the failure to follow the Scheduled Maintenance Checklists provided with this guarantee." — Source: **GAF (Diamond Pledge NDL guarantee, COMTS700).** (This same primary language also covers the "deferred maintenance / unauthorized alteration voids coverage" point — see §3; do NOT cite Angi.)
- **Leak-notice deadline + investigation-cost provision.** GAF's Diamond Pledge NDL requires the owner to notify GAF **in writing within 30 days** of a leak through the GAF materials; if investigation shows the leak is **not** covered, the owner agrees to pay a **$500 investigation cost** or the guarantee is cancelled. — Source: **GAF (Diamond Pledge NDL guarantee, COMTS700).** **(This $500 is the leak-investigation cost — NOT the NJ contract threshold in §4.)**

### 2.5 Single-ply term ranges (KEEP the JM range; HEDGE the rest per critic)
- **Single-ply (TPO/EPDM/PVC) NDL terms commonly run 5 to 30 years**, driven by membrane thickness and system spec. Johns Manville Peak Advantage guarantees "range in coverage time from 5 to 30 years" across its single-ply and bituminous systems, all issued as NDL. — Source: **Johns Manville (Peak Advantage Guarantees, RS-7377).**
- **Some single-ply NDL guarantees extend to 35 years via a maintenance-based extension.** GAF EverGuard runs "Up to 35 Years (for qualifying systems)"; a guarantee up to 30 years can be extended (to a 35-yr max) through the **WellRoof Guarantee Extension** only when a Certified Maintenance Professional performs annual inspection/maintenance and submits documentation. — Source: **GAF (Commercial Guarantee Comparison Guide; WellRoof Guarantee Extension addendum).**

  - **Holcim Elevate (formerly Firestone Building Products) Red Shield** provides "material and labor coverage with comprehensive, no-dollar limit liability" and "edge-to-edge system coverage including membrane, insulation and metal," transferable owner-to-owner, **5 years up to 30 years** by system. — Source: **Holcim Elevate / Firestone Red Shield warranty materials.** HEDGE: drop the brand-history aside and the "Platinum 30-year" tier label unless re-verified on Holcim's current warranty page; present as "up to 30 years."

## 3. What voids a warranty + transferability — NEW (named sources; weak aggregators DROPPED/re-attributed)

### 3.1 Inadequate attic ventilation — the most-cited shingle-warranty void cause (KEEP)
- **Inadequate attic ventilation is the most-cited cause of voided shingle warranties.** GAF warranties require attic ventilation meeting local code or GAF's specifications; when shingles fail prematurely (curling, cracking, blistering, sealant-strip failure) GAF can attribute the failure to ventilation and deny the claim — including when intake vents are painted over or blocked by insulation. — Source: **GAF (residential warranty terms).**
- **The technical mechanism:** "Poor ventilation of the roof structure shortens shingle lifespan by failing to keep shingles cool, resulting in accelerated loss of volatiles and greater amounts of thermal cycling." — Source: **InterNACHI (Mastering Roof Inspections: Asphalt Composition Shingles, Part 30).**
- **The code basis is IRC R806.2:** minimum net free ventilating area = **1/150 of the vented space**, reducible to **1/300** only when a Class I/II vapor retarder is installed (Climate Zones 6–8) AND **40–50% of the ventilating area is in the upper portion** (within 3 ft of the ridge — exhaust) with the balance in the bottom one-third (eave/soffit intake). — Source: **International Residential Code (IRC) 2021, Section R806.2 "Minimum vent area."** (Newark = IECC Climate Zone 4A, so the 1/300 reduction's CZ 6–8 condition generally does NOT apply — present 1/150 as the working NJ baseline and note the 1/300 reduction is conditional.)

### 3.2 Installer credentials + registration (KEEP; VERIFY currency on the 2026 policy line)
- **Enhanced/premium warranty tiers require a credentialed installer.** GAF's Golden Pledge limited warranty can only be offered by **Master Elite contractors (the top ~3% of GAF-certified contractors)**. — Source: **GAF (Golden Pledge / Master Elite program terms).**
- The claim that "as of 2026 only GAF-certified contractors can register workmanship warranties with GAF" is a **program-policy statement** tied only to GAF's generic residential page — **VERIFY against current GAF terms before publishing as present-tense.** The Master Elite "~top 3%" and "Golden Pledge requires Master Elite" facts are stable and safe to publish.

### 3.3 Solar/PV, alterations, deferred maintenance (KEEP substance; DROP weak citations)
- **Mounting solar/satellite does not automatically void coverage, but improper penetration/flashing does.** GAF's technical guidance states its warranties remain in place when a PV system is installed over GAF roofing products provided manufacturer details are followed; improper flashing, non-approved sealants, or installation damage voids coverage for that damage, and the cost to remove a solar system to inspect defective shingles is not covered. — Source: **GAF (technical guidance on PV systems over GAF roofing).** HEDGE per critic: attribute to GAF's own bulletin, NOT QuickBOLT; if the primary can't be located, drop the specific removal-cost detail.
- **Unauthorized repairs/modifications and deferred maintenance are standard void causes** — manufacturers deny claims tied to alteration or neglect rather than a manufacturing defect. **Source this to the GAF/JM "Owner Responsibilities / Scheduled Maintenance Checklist" exclusion language in §2.4, NOT to Angi** (critic DROP — Angi is a lead-gen aggregator and the point is already covered by the primary owner-responsibility text).

### 3.4 Manufacturer vs. contractor warranty — the load-bearing distinction (KEEP; re-attribute)
- **A manufacturer warranty covers material/product defects and survives the installing contractor closing; a contractor workmanship warranty covers installation quality and is only as good as the contractor's continued solvency** — a contractor who ceases operations cannot fulfill workmanship obligations. This is the single most decision-relevant point in the void set. — Re-attribute to **NRCA guidance / the NRCA Roofing Manual** (and corroborated by InterNACHI/IIBEC), **NOT** to "National Roofing Authority" (critic flag: a look-alike site, not the NRCA).
- **Contractor workmanship terms vary widely with no industry-mandated minimum.** Reconcile the conflicting researcher numbers to a single published figure: **commonly 1–10 years, no industry standard requires longer** (NRCA, via facts-causes-signs line 41 which already states "often only 1–2 years"). Do NOT publish a fabricated "~2-year average." A contractor workmanship warranty covers installation defects (improperly welded seams, poorly sealed flashing, fastener problems), distinct from factory-defect material coverage. — Source: **NRCA; Owens Corning Roofing (three-types framing).** HEDGE per critic: keep the qualitative install-defect list; soften the numbers as above.

### 3.5 Transferability mechanics (LEAD with the GAF concrete example; HEDGE generic windows)
- **General pattern:** most manufacturer warranties transfer **only once** (original owner → first subsequent buyer); the new owner must register the transfer within a stated window (varies by manufacturer); some reduce coverage for the second owner. — Source: **NRCIA.** HEDGE per critic: present the generic 30–60-day window as "varies by manufacturer," NOT as universal.
- **Concrete example (lead with this):** GAF's Golden Pledge limited warranty may be transferred **only once**; if the transfer occurs **within the first 20 years** after installation the second owner gets the original coverage, but **after 20 years it is reduced to a 2-year period** after the ownership change, and the transfer must be registered within the stated post-sale window. — Source: **GAF (Golden Pledge Limited Warranty terms).** **VERIFY the 20-yr/2-yr split against the actual Golden Pledge warranty PDF before publishing** (critic: currently pinned only to GAF's generic landing page). Cross-reference the verified CertainTeed example already in hand: **SureStart PLUS is fully transferable if sold within 15 years** (facts-process-standards line 145).
- **Missing registration downgrades coverage:** premium tiers typically require registration within a set window (commonly ~60 days) to activate the full/extended tier; without it only the standard limited warranty applies and transferability may be limited. — Source: **NRCIA / industry warranty guidance.** HEDGE: frame the window as manufacturer-specific (e.g., CertainTeed's 60 days, line 145), not universal.

## 4. NJ written-warranty / consumer law — POINTERS + the warranty-disclosure hook (reuse CMP-3 corrected framing — do NOT reintroduce the errors)

This is the page's local-SEO reason to exist. **Reuse the exact corrected framing from CMP-3 (GAP-service-decisions.md §2 / lines 18–72) — do not re-derive.** Two separate requirements, never conflated:

- **HIC registration = $0 floor.** Any business selling/performing home improvements (including roofing) must register annually with the **NJ Division of Consumer Affairs** under the **Contractors' Registration Act, N.J.S.A. 56:8-136 et seq.** — **no dollar threshold.** It is a **registration, not a "license"** (NJ has no general HIC license — never write "licensed HIC"). Source: **NJ Division of Consumer Affairs (Contractors' Registration Act).** Pointer: facts-nj-regulatory-climate.md lines 81–114.
- **The $500 figure = the WRITTEN-CONTRACT trigger, and it is the warranty-disclosure hook.** Under the Consumer Fraud Act's home-improvement regulation **N.J.A.C. 13:45A-16.2(a)12**, every home-improvement contract "for a purchase price in excess of $500.00" must be in a signed writing — and its enumerated required elements include **"any guarantee or warranty"** the contractor provides (element vi, **N.J.A.C. 13:45A-16.2(a)12(vi)**). State, with attribution, that **NJ requires the contractor's warranty terms to be disclosed in the written contract.** Source: **N.J.A.C. 13:45A-16.2(a)12.** (The registration-number-on-contract requirement is a SEPARATE provision — cite to **N.J.A.C. 13:45A-17.13** / the Act if mentioned at all, NOT to 16.2.)
- **Homeowner-on-own-home is exempt from HIC registration** (**N.J.S.A. 56:8-140**) — but local permits and the NJ Uniform Construction Code (**N.J.A.C. 5:23**) may still apply.
- **Recourse:** disputes route through the **NJ Division of Consumer Affairs, Office of Consumer Protection.** Pointer: facts-nj-regulatory-climate.md line 81.
- **Federal backstop — Magnuson-Moss "limited warranty":** every manufacturer document here is titled a "Limited Warranty/Guarantee." Under the federal **Magnuson-Moss Warranty Act**, "full" vs. "limited" is a labeling term of art — a **"limited" warranty signals the coverage is conditioned/restricted** (e.g., prorated terms, owner-maintenance obligations, exclusions) rather than unconditional. The page should define why these are *limited* so the word isn't left unexplained. Source: **Magnuson-Moss Warranty Act (15 U.S.C. §2301 et seq.).** (Use to frame, not to over-claim — do not invent specific federal disclosure dollar figures.)
- **Do NOT reintroduce CMP-3 errors:** the $500 is the written-contract trigger, NOT a registration threshold; "registration," not "license"; the Dec. 31, 2005 date is the registration-mandate effective date, NOT the $500-rule date — keep it out of published copy.

## 5. Cross-page pointers for the other 7 decision-helpers (so authors do NOT re-research)

### 5.1 Reflectance / cool-roof (use these figures; do NOT invent reflectance %)
Pointer: **facts-energy-solar.md** §0 master corrections + the PRIMARY table (lines 70–77, 263–270).
- Cool roofs are defined by **solar reflectance + thermal emittance per CRRC-1**, NOT R-value — coatings/reflective surfaces **add NO R-value** (DOE/CRRC/RCMA). The ENERGY STAR roof program is dead (ended June 1, 2022); cite **CRRC**, never ENERGY STAR.
- **EPA:** a cool roof's reflectance can reduce **PEAK cooling demand by 11–27%** in air-conditioned residential buildings — this is PEAK demand, **NOT an annual bill %.** Never restate as "cuts your energy bill 11–27%."
- **DOE:** a reflective roof stays **>50°F cooler** than a conventional roof on a sunny summer afternoon.
- **LBNL:** a clean white roof (80% reflective) stays **~55°F / 31°C cooler** than a gray roof (20% reflective).
- **Federal:** §25D residential solar credit + §25C insulation credit both **repealed after Dec 31, 2025** (P.L. 119-21 / OBBB); §179D is a commercial deduction, not a roof credit. Source: IRS.

### 5.2 Historic credits / COA (use these; do NOT invent eligibility)
Pointer: **facts-historic-restoration.md** §0/§10 (lines 280–286).
- The binding private-owner gate for a reroof is the **local Certificate of Appropriateness (COA)** from a municipal Historic Preservation Commission under **N.J.S.A. 40:55D-107** (et seq., through -112) — required before regulated alterations to a designated landmark or a property in a LOCAL historic district. A National Register listing **alone** is NOT the binding gate (NPS: owners may use Register-listed property "within the framework of local laws or ordinances"). A COA does NOT replace a building permit.
- **§47 Historic Tax Credit = income-producing properties only** (not owner-occupied homes). **§25D/§25C repealed** (P.L. 119-21). The NJ historic-credit bill **S3545 is NOT law.** Source: NPS; IRS; N.J.S.A. 40:55D-107.
