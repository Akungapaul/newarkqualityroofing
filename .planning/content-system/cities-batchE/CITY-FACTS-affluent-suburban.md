# Affluent-Suburban Essex County Cities — Fact Bank (Cities Batch E)

House style = **name-only attribution** in prose (cite the body by name; no outbound
links / URLs in on-page copy). The two cities here share one NJ UCC permit rule and one
climate baseline — do NOT re-derive either; cross-reference the shared packs. What
differs city-to-city is the **construction office**, the **historic COA gate** (which
diverges sharply between these two — read Rule 1 carefully), the **Census housing
profile**, the **verified neighborhoods**, and the **geography (Watchung-ridge vs.
Passaic-floodplain edge)**.

These are the **affluent-suburban archetype**: two high-wealth, overwhelmingly
owner-occupied Essex County communities with a deep stock of premium/heritage roof
systems and a shared **mature street-tree canopy** roofing stressor framed
QUALITATIVELY (leaf/debris clogging valleys and gutters, branch-impact in nor'easters
and summer storms, shade-driven moss/algae on north-facing slopes). The two diverge on
the single highest-risk fact in this whole batch — the **historic COA gate**:
**Millburn HAS a BINDING local Historic Preservation Commission and ordinance (Article
8) that issues Certificates of Appropriateness — but ONLY within its locally designated
districts (Wyoming, Short Hills Park) or on an individually designated landmark, NOT
township-wide**; **Livingston has NO binding local historic gate at all** (its Master
Plan only RECOMMENDS that the township "consider" adopting one). They also diverge on
geography: **Millburn** abuts **South Mountain Reservation** (Watchung-foothills ridge
terrain) and its **downtown village floods** along the Rahway River; **Livingston**
carries a localized **Passaic-River / Willow Brook** floodplain on its **western edge**
only and hosts the **West Essex Park** greenway and **Riker Hill Art Park**. Never
cross-attribute these geographic features between the two.

### 1. HISTORIC COA GATE per city (CRITICAL — the binding gate is the LOCAL ordinance + COA from the municipal HPC, NOT Register listing, NOT a township/society-owned site, NOT a museum)

**The binding historic gate is the LOCAL ordinance + COA from the municipal HPC, NOT
National/State Register listing, NOT a township/state-owned historic site, and NOT a
historical-society museum.** Per the National Park Service, "Listing in the National
Register places no federal restrictions on a private property owner" — Register listing
alone never restricts a private reroof. The two cities here are OPPOSITE ends of the
spectrum; do NOT flatten them, and do NOT swap their rules.

- **Millburn = YES — a BINDING local HPC + ordinance issues Certificates of
  Appropriateness, but ONLY within designated districts/landmarks, NOT township-wide.**
  The Township of Millburn Historic Preservation Commission was created by Ordinance
  10-87 (1987), re-established/refined by Ordinances 8-89, 10-89 and 2470-16, and
  codified as **ARTICLE 8 of the Township of Millburn Development Regulations and Zoning
  Ordinance** (authorized under NJ MLUL N.J.S.A. 40:55D-107 et seq. / 40:55D-65.1). It is
  **BINDING, not advisory**: per the ordinance, "A Certificate of Appropriateness ...
  issued by the Commission shall be required before any work is commenced on any historic
  landmark or within any historic district." **CRITICAL SCOPE: the COA requirement is NOT
  township-wide.** It applies ONLY to a property that is a designated historic landmark OR
  located within a locally designated historic district. The township's own examples of
  permit-triggering exterior work requiring a COA EXPLICITLY include "roof repairs or
  replacement." Owners check property status via the township SDL Portal. Correct
  framing: "Most Millburn and Short Hills homes need NO Historic Preservation Commission
  review, but a home that is an individually designated landmark or inside the Wyoming or
  Short Hills Park historic district must obtain a Certificate of Appropriateness from the
  Millburn HPC before permit-triggering exterior or roof work." Do NOT write the blanket
  "a COA is required to reroof in Millburn/Short Hills" — and do NOT under-state it as
  advisory-only; it is binding WITHIN those districts/landmarks.
  - **Two CONFIRMED locally designated districts (COA APPLIES):** the **Wyoming Historic
    District** (established by Ordinance 10-89; in the Wyoming section, one of the
    township's earliest residential neighborhoods, includes the Wyoming Presbyterian
    Church) and the **Short Hills Park Historic District** (Millburn's other officially
    designated LOCAL district AND National-Register-listed Sept 18, 1980 / NJ Register
    Feb 1, 1980; ~1,000 acres, ~125 mostly-residential buildings; the romantic planned
    suburb founded 1877 by Stewart Hartshorn; Queen Anne / Shingle / revival styles). The
    LOCAL designation is what triggers a COA — the Register listing alone would not
    restrict a private owner (NPS). Do NOT present Short Hills Park's 1980 NRHP listing as
    the thing that gates a reroof.
  - **Short Hills Village = a recently designated / pending THIRD district — VERIFY current
    status; do NOT flatly deny a COA there.** The Millburn HPC unanimously recommended
    designating Short Hills Village (HPC vote ~03/07/2024); designating **Ordinance
    2667-24** (bounded roughly by Forest Drive South, Chatham Road, Short Hills Avenue,
    Woodland Road, Crescent Park/Place, Station Plaza, Baltusrol Way) cleared its public
    hearing ~04/16/2024 and **no longer appears on Millburn's Proposed-Ordinances page**
    (which removes ordinances once adopted/codified), with at least one secondary source
    calling Short Hills Village the township's third/newest historic district. No primary
    Township Committee final-vote record was confirmed either way. CORRECT framing:
    "Short Hills Village is a recently designated or pending third historic district —
    verify its current designation status; if designated, a COA applies there too." Do NOT
    assert "no COA in Short Hills Village" as settled, and do NOT assert a COA there as
    settled either.
  - **Individually designated LANDMARKS (Article 8 Schedule A) ALSO trigger a COA — list
    not captured.** Beyond the two districts, Article 8 contemplates individually
    designated landmark PARCELS that ALSO require a COA for permit-triggering roof work.
    No researcher pulled the Schedule-A landmark list, so its count/identity is UNVERIFIED.
    Frame COA scope as "the Wyoming or Short Hills Park district OR any individually
    designated landmark," not just the two districts.
  - **Register-only / institutional assets are NOT homeowner COA gates:** the **Hessian
    House**, the **Cora Hartshorn Arboretum** (16-acre arboretum / bird sanctuary on
    Forest Drive, Short Hills), and the **Paper Mill Playhouse** (1,200-seat regional
    theater, est. 1938) are notable heritage assets, but a Register listing or an
    institutional building imposes NO COA on a neighboring private homeowner's reroof —
    only LOCAL designation (Wyoming, Short Hills Park, or an individually designated
    landmark) does. Heritage color ONLY.
  - **COA fees (time-sensitive):** $200 COA application fee plus a $750 escrow fee for
    applications going to the full Commission; the township states district designation
    itself adds NO additional taxes/fees except the COA fee when making exterior changes.
    Fees/titles are time-sensitive — verify current before quoting.
- **Livingston = NO — no binding local HPC, no ordinance, no COA, for private homes.**
  Livingston's own Master Plan **Historic Preservation Plan Element (Section XI)** only
  RECOMMENDS that "the Township should consider, pursuant to N.J.S. 40:55D-65.1, amending
  the Land Use Ordinance to establish provisions that might encourage preserving, and
  discourage demolition, of historic buildings" — i.e., the township has **NOT adopted a
  binding preservation ordinance with COA review over private property.** The element's
  approach is voluntary/incentive-based (encourage Register listing, tax relief,
  easements, identifying signs). The element itself quotes the principle that a NJ-Register
  listing "does not place limitations upon a private owner's property rights. Neither does
  a listing on the Federal register," and that the MLUL merely ENABLES (does not require) a
  municipality to adopt regulating ordinances. Correct framing: "Livingston has not
  designated any local historic district or landmark that requires a Certificate of
  Appropriateness, so a private homeowner's reroof in Livingston is not subject to a
  municipal historic-design-review gate."
  - **Do NOT treat the §170-3 "Historic site" definition or the 38 Master-Plan-identified
    sites as a COA trigger.** Code §170-3 defines "Historic site" as property "formally
    designated in the Master Plan"; the Master Plan identifies 38 such sites "for
    municipal planning purposes" (up from 27 in 2007). These are Master-Plan PLANNING
    identifications, NOT local-ordinance designations that bind a private owner. Do NOT
    treat the §170-3 label or the 38-site list as a reroof gate.
  - **Force Homestead (Ward–Force House / Condit Family Cook House) = TOWNSHIP-OWNED
    MUSEUM, Register-listed — NOT a homeowner COA gate.** Purchased by the Township in
    1962; the two buildings were listed together on the NJ and National Registers in 1981;
    operated as a museum / Livingston Historical Society headquarters at 366 S. Livingston
    Avenue (closed since March 2023 for multi-year restoration). A township-owned,
    Register-listed museum imposes NO rule on any private neighbor's roof. Heritage color
    ONLY. The Livingston Historical Society (founded 1963) is a nonprofit with NO
    regulatory authority over private property.
  - The only clearly confirmed National/State Register property in Livingston is the Force
    Homestead (listed 1981) — and it is township-owned. Other Master-Plan list sites (e.g.
    "Crystal Plaza," named churches/cemeteries) have UNCONFIRMED Register status — omit or
    treat as local historic interest only, never as COA gates.

### 2. CONSTRUCTION / PERMIT OFFICE per city

- **Both cities** apply the same statewide UCC rule (cross-ref
  facts-nj-regulatory-climate.md §1): a **detached one- or two-family dwelling reroof —
  including a full tear-off and replacement of the roof covering — is "ordinary
  maintenance" under N.J.A.C. 5:23-2.7 and requires NO construction permit, no inspection,
  and no notice.** A permit IS required on commercial, multi-family, or attached buildings
  (the 25% rule) and for any structural roof work; recover-vs-tear-off is governed by
  Rehab Subcode 5:23-6.4. Each city enforces but does not override the state
  classification. **A COA on a locally designated Millburn property is a SEPARATE
  approval** — not a substitute for, nor a trigger of, a building permit; a 1–2 family
  reroof stays UCC "ordinary maintenance" even where a COA is required.
- **Livingston** — the **Township of Livingston Building Department** (administers the NJ
  UCC: building, electric, fire and plumbing permits, plan review, inspections, and
  Certificates of Occupancy), at **Livingston Town Hall, 357 S. Livingston Avenue,
  Livingston NJ 07039**. A named Construction Code Official, **Martin J. Chiarolanzio**,
  is sourced (Township .gov Building Department page) — title is time-sensitive; verify
  before printing. The large commercial stock (Route 10, Eisenhower Parkway office/medical
  parks, Livingston Town Center, the Cooperman Barnabas hospital campus) is where the 25%
  rule + permit applies.
- **Millburn** — the **Township of Millburn Building Department** (enforces the Township
  Zoning Ordinance and administers the NJ UCC; the UCC is administered by the township
  Construction Official with Building, Electrical, Plumbing and Fire subcode
  officials/inspectors). **ADDRESS IS UNSETTLED — VERIFY BEFORE PRINTING:** the older
  reference is "375 Millburn Avenue, first floor of Town Hall," BUT the township's own
  live Building Department page lists **"22 East Willow St., Millburn NJ 07041" (phone
  973-564-7055)** as the (apparently relocated/temporary) Building Department office. 375
  Millburn Ave is Town Hall; 22 East Willow St is the current permit/COA-filing location
  per the township's current page — do NOT print "375 Millburn Avenue" as the filing
  address without live verification. The current **Construction Official NAME is NOT
  sourced** (Livingston has one; Millburn does not) — name the FUNCTION; if the pages name
  the Livingston official, parity requires naming Millburn's or naming neither. Public
  hours (Mon–Fri 8:30 a.m.–3:30 p.m.) and phone are time-sensitive. The 25% rule + permit
  applies to the Mall at Short Hills, the downtown Millburn retail/restaurant district,
  and professional offices.

### 3. RESERVATION / COUNTY-PARK GEOGRAPHY corrected (verified — do NOT misattribute)

- **South Mountain Reservation (~2,112 ac, between the First and Second Watchung ridges)**
  extends through **West Orange, Maplewood, and MILLBURN** (and borders South Orange).
  Among the two affluent-suburban cities, **ONLY Millburn** abuts South Mountain
  Reservation. **Do NOT attribute South Mountain Reservation to Livingston** (Livingston
  does not lie in or border it).
- **West Essex Park (a ~1,360-acre Essex County Passaic-River wetlands preserve /
  greenway)**, **Riker Hill Art Park**, and (partly) **Becker Park** are LIVINGSTON-side
  county assets. West Essex Park stretches ~6 miles along the Passaic through Livingston,
  Roseland, West Caldwell and Fairfield, ending "just beyond South Orange Avenue in
  Livingston." **Riker Hill Art Park** (42 acres, 276 Beaufort Avenue, Livingston — a
  former U.S. Missile Tracking / Nike radar base bought from the federal government for $1
  in 1974; now 38 artist studios) is a county ART PARK in Livingston — name it a county
  art park, NOT a reservation. **Becker Park** (146.73-acre Essex County tract off Eagle
  Rock Avenue) lies partly in Livingston and partly in Roseland.
- **Walter Kidde Dinosaur Park / Riker Hill Fossil Site is in ROSELAND, NOT Livingston.**
  The 16-acre site (National Natural Landmark since June 1971; 1,000+ Late-Triassic /
  Early-Jurassic tracks; former Nike-site quarry) sits at the Roseland–Livingston border
  on the southwestern side of Roseland; best access is Locust Ave in Roseland. Do NOT
  attribute the fossil site / dinosaur park to Livingston — the Livingston-side county
  assets are Riker Hill ART Park and (partly) Becker Park.
- **Do NOT cross-attribute:** do NOT place South Mountain Reservation in Livingston, and
  do NOT attribute West Essex Park / Riker Hill / any Passaic-River feature to Millburn —
  those belong to the Livingston / west-Essex side.

### 4. FLOOD FRAMING — Millburn DOWNTOWN (Rahway River) and Livingston WESTERN EDGE (Passaic / Willow Brook) ONLY; a roof-relevant DRAINAGE/storm stressor

- **Millburn DOWNTOWN flash-floods along the Rahway River — frame as a roof-relevant
  storm/drainage stressor for the DOWNTOWN low-slope COMMERCIAL roofs.** The downtown
  Millburn village sits on the Rahway River and has repeatedly flash-flooded:
  **Hurricane/Tropical Storm Ida (Sept 1–2, 2021)** severely flooded downtown (Millburn
  Avenue sections closed; floodwaters overflowed into downtown businesses); local coverage
  cites three major events in ~20 years — **Hurricane Floyd (1999)**, **Hurricane Irene
  (2011)**, and **Ida (2021)**. Frame as positive low-slope drainage, sound flashing, and
  gutter/downspout/scupper capacity on the downtown low-slope commercial roofs plus
  general storm resilience — **NOT a basement/interior-flood claim**, NOT township-wide,
  and NOT a residential-Short-Hills claim (Short Hills sits on higher ridge ground). NO
  FEMA Special-Flood-Hazard-Area designation for the downtown corridor was sourced — frame
  the downtown as a documented flash-flood / storm-drainage corridor QUALITATIVELY, not
  via a cited FEMA zone. River-branch note: Wikipedia and Patch both identify it as the
  WEST Branch of the Rahway River; the only "East Branch" claim is from the original draft
  brief. Write simply "the Rahway River" to be safe.
- **Livingston's western / low-lying EDGE carries a localized Passaic-River / Willow Brook
  floodplain only.** Livingston's western municipal edge runs along the Passaic River. The
  Township's flood-hazard code (Ch. 170 flood-hazard-area provisions) and the Essex County
  FEMA Flood Insurance Study describe Passaic River and Canoe Brook flood reaches; the
  Essex County Multi-Jurisdictional Hazard Mitigation Plan names **"Willow Brook in
  Livingston"** as the local floodplain feature (and ties Canoe / Slough Brooks more
  strongly to MILLBURN Township). LEAD the Livingston flood framing with the **Passaic
  River western edge (and Willow Brook)**; do NOT present Canoe Brook as the primary
  Livingston watercourse. Frame QUALITATIVELY as a localized roof-relevant
  DRAINAGE/storm-resilience stressor for the western/low-lying edge — **NOT a township-wide
  flood claim and NOT a basement/interior-flood claim** (most of Livingston, including
  upland eastern sections like Riker Hill, is not in the floodplain). Precise FEMA zone
  labels (AE vs. A) / BFEs were not extracted — keep flood framing qualitative and
  edge-localized.
