## 0. CRITICAL CORRECTIONS (read first)

- **It is "OSHA" or "federal OSHA," NOT "NJ OSHA."** New Jersey has **no state OSHA plan for the private sector** — federal OSHA covers all private NJ workplaces. NJ's state plan (PEOSH) covers **public/government employees only.** Never write "NJ OSHA regulates DIY work" or imply NJ runs its own OSHA over contractors.
- **OSHA's fall-protection rules bind EMPLOYERS, not homeowners.** Frame OSHA as applying to professional roofing crews (their employer's duty). A homeowner repairing their own roof is **outside OSHA jurisdiction entirely** — which is the point: they work with no harness, no anchor, no safety-trained crew.
- **Lead homeowner fall-safety with CONSUMER data, not worker data.** Use the peer-reviewed ladder-injury figure (D'Souza et al., ~136,000/yr, 97.3% non-occupational). Worker fatality data (BLS CFOI 2023) may be used ONLY as a clearly-labeled "even among trained professionals…" contrast — never applied to homeowners.
- **HIC registration ≠ the $500 figure.** These are TWO separate requirements, and the draft conflates them:
  - **Registration** (Contractors' Registration Act, N.J.S.A. 56:8-136) applies to any business doing home improvements with **NO dollar threshold ($0 floor).** It is a **registration, not a "license"** — NJ has no general HIC license. Avoid "licensed HIC."
  - **The $500 figure** is the **written-contract trigger** under the Consumer Fraud Act home-improvement regulation **N.J.A.C. 13:45A-16.2(a)12** — contracts "in excess of $500.00" must be a signed writing. It is NOT a registration trigger.
- **Homeowner-on-own-home is exempt from HIC registration** (N.J.S.A. 56:8-140) — but local permits and the NJ Uniform Construction Code may still apply.
- **DROP the "$1 saves $3–$5" maintenance ROI ratio.** Un-sourced contractor folklore with no roof-specific (or even building-specific) primary study. **Do not reframe it as fact, and do not substitute DOE/FEMP's "12–18%" as a stand-in ratio.** Reframe qualitatively (small early fix vs. large deferred cost).
- **DROP the "$250–$600/yr maintenance cost" figure** — no named source. Replace with the Angi 2026 roof-INSPECTION benchmark ($249 avg; $75–$400) or, cleaner, free-inspection framing.
- **NEVER attribute the "21 vs 13 year" lifespan figure to the NRCA.** Its real primary source is Roofing Contractor magazine (Nov 2009) and it is **commercial low-slope roof data.** Same caution for the companion 14¢/25¢-per-sq-ft figure.
- **DROP the "5–10 additional years" NRCA-attributed residential figure** — LOW confidence, no primary NRCA doc (sourced to a home-inspector site). This is a second "doubles/extends lifespan" smell.
