# Roof Repair — Causes & Warning Signs

Fact base for the NQR Roof Repair pilot page (causes of roof damage + observable
warning signs + component failure points). Numbers and named sources are cited
inline. Anything that could not be sourced to a named authority is marked
**[UNVERIFIED]**. House style is **name-only attribution** (cite the org by name
in prose; no outbound links) — the URLs in §8 are for the writer's verification.

> **Sourcing caution carried throughout:** the widely-circulated "90–95% of roof
> leaks start at flashing" figure is a **secondary-source / trade-consensus**
> number, NOT a published primary-source statistic from NRCA. It is consistent
> across many independent trade sources and is broadly accepted in the industry,
> but it must be framed as "the roofing industry estimates" / "most roofing pros
> say," never as a precise, primary-cited NRCA dataset. See §2.1.

## 1. Baseline: how long an asphalt-shingle roof should last

| Fact | Value | Source / status |
|---|---|---|
| 3-tab asphalt shingle service life | **~15–20 years** under standard conditions | National Roofing Authority summarizing NRCA/ARMA service-life guidance |
| Architectural (dimensional) shingle service life | **~25–30 years** under standard conditions | National Roofing Authority (NRCA/ARMA guidance); corroborated by GAF for zone-5 climates |
| Asphalt-shingle share of U.S. residential roofing | **~73%** of the market (2024) | Roofing industry market analyses (trade data — treat as approximate) |
| Manufacturer warranty vs. real service life | Labels of "30-yr / 40-yr / lifetime" describe the **warranty period**, not guaranteed functional life; most are **limited + pro-rated**, covering only manufacturing defects (not install errors, storms, or neglect) | NRCA; trade analyses |
| Contractor workmanship warranty | Often only **1–2 years**; no industry standard requires longer | NRCA |

> Why it matters for Newark homeowners: a roof showing heavy wear well before the
> 15–30-yr window suggests **premature failure** (install defect, ventilation,
> or storm) — a repairable/diagnosable event, not just "old age."

## 2. Common causes of roof damage & leaks

### 2.1 Flashing failure — the #1 leak source

- The roofing industry estimates that **roughly 90–95% of roof leaks originate at
  flashing details** — chimneys, walls/dormers, skylights, valleys, and
  penetrations — **not** through the open shingle field. By implication, only
  **~5–10% of leaks** are primarily a field-shingle failure.
  **Source: trade-consensus figure attributed to NRCA across multiple roofing
  sources. ⚠️ Secondary-source — frame as an industry estimate, not a primary
  NRCA dataset.**
- **GAF** technical guidance: poorly manufactured and installed flashings are
  cited as a leading cause of roof leaks. Source: GAF (via trade summaries).
- Flashing failure modes: corrosion/rust (worse in coastal/industrial air),
  thermal expansion cracking sealant laps, wind lifting/bending, inadequate
  overlaps or fasteners, and **sealant/caulk that typically fails in 5–10 years**.
  Source: roofing trade guidance (WeatherShield; Enterprise Roofing).
- **Pipe boots** (the rubber/neoprene collar at vent stacks) are the single most
  common penetration failure point: a quality boot lasts **~10–15 years**, but
  one installed with exposed nails and no ice-and-water shield can fail in
  **2–5 years**. Source: roofing contractor guidance (Dom Roofing).

### 2.2 Wind uplift & storm damage

- **NOAA** classifies a thunderstorm as *severe* at **wind gusts ≥ 58 mph
  (50 knots)**. Source: NOAA / National Weather Service (Storm Events Database).
- Standard **3-tab shingles** are commonly rated to about **60 mph** (≈ ASTM
  D3161 Class A); many **architectural shingles** are warrantied to **130 mph**
  with enhanced (6-nail) installation. Source: ARMA/manufacturer guidance; AIC
  Roofing summarizing ASTM classes.
- Wind-resistance test classes (for any "wind-rated" claim — use only if NQR
  verifies the product): **ASTM D3161** Class A = 60 mph, Class D = 90 mph,
  **Class F = 110 mph**; **ASTM D7158** (uplift method, 3-sec gust Vult)
  Class D ≈ 116 mph, **Class G ≈ 150–155 mph, Class H ≈ 190–194 mph**.
  Source: ASTM D3161 / D7158; NRCA *Professional Roofing*; AIC Roofing.
- **Mechanism:** uplift is highest at **roof edges, rakes, and corners**; damage
  starts there. Source: IIBEC; trade wind-damage guidance.
- **IBHS** field-aging finding: the share of **partially unsealed** field shingles
  rises sharply with roof age — **<1%** on roofs 0–6 years old vs. **>79%** on
  roofs 14–20 years old — and these pre-unsealed patterns match post-storm
  blow-off patterns. Source: IBHS / University of Florida in-situ shingle study
  (27 roofs).
- **IBHS** wind testing: the **strength of the seal between shingles** is the most
  important high-wind factor; seals are heat-activated, so **winter/cold or
  dusty installs** can leave shingles weakly sealed and prone to blow-off.
  Source: IBHS wind-uplift pilot study.

### 2.3 Hail impact

- **NOAA** severe-hail warning threshold: **¾ inch (0.75 in) diameter or larger**.
  Source: NOAA / NWS Storm Events Database.
- Roofing-damage threshold is **larger** than the warning threshold. Lab testing
  (ice-ball launcher) found functional damage begins at about: **1.0 in** for
  aged 3-tab shingles, **~1.25 in** for fiberglass-mat / most common products,
  **~1.5 in** for thicker laminated shingles, **~1.75 in** for concrete tile;
  **all** tested roofing damaged by **2.0 in** hail. Source: American
  Meteorological Society (AMS) conference paper on hail damage thresholds
  (Marshall et al.) — peer-reviewed atmospheric-science source.
- **IBHS** Impact-Resistant (IR) Shingle Ratings program tested **22 asphalt
  shingle products from 5 manufacturers** using **1.5- and 2.0-inch
  laboratory-made hailstones**. Source: IBHS (directly verified on ibhs.org).
  *(Note: one secondary source said "24 products" — IBHS's own page says 22; use
  22.)*
- **IBHS** notes hail damage depends on **kinetic energy = hail size + wind
  speed**, so a 0.75-in stone in high wind can outdamage a 1-in stone in calm
  air. Source: IBHS hail research.
- **UL 2218** impact rating (steel-ball drop): products rated **Class 1–4**,
  Class 4 most resistant; FLASH/IBHS recommend **Class 3 or 4** impact-resistant
  shingles in hail-prone areas. Source: UL 2218; FLASH; IBHS.
- Hail-damage field signs: **circular "bruises" with granule loss**, mat
  exposure, soft/spongy spots; denting on metal gutters/vents. IBHS-style
  insurer protocols often use **8 functional impacts per 100 sq ft** as a
  damage benchmark. Source: IBHS / insurer hail-assessment protocols.

### 2.4 Ice dams (high-relevance for Newark/NJ winters)

- **Mechanism (University of Minnesota Extension):** three conditions — (1) snow
  on the roof; (2) upper roof surface **above 32°F** (melting the snowpack from
  beneath); (3) eave/lower roof **below 32°F** (refreezing the meltwater into a
  dam at the edge). Trapped water then backs up **under the shingles** and into
  the home. Source: University of Minnesota Extension.
- **Root cause is attic heat escape**, driven more by **air leakage/bypasses**
  than insulation alone — "adding insulation without air-sealing is like putting
  a blanket over a fan." Source: building-science / ice-dam contractor guidance.
- Cold-climate insulation targets cited for ice-dam resistance: **R-49 to R-60**
  attic insulation (≈ 16–20 in blown-in); some older code minimums (R-38/R-44)
  are considered insufficient. Source: cold-climate ice-dam specialists (note:
  Minnesota-code figures; **NJ-specific code value is [UNVERIFIED]** — confirm
  against the NJ Uniform Construction Code / IECC zone for Essex County before
  citing a number to NJ homeowners).
- **Observable ice-dam warning signs:** large icicles at the eaves/gutters,
  thick ice ridges at the roof edge, **uneven snow-melt** (bare patches where
  attic heat escapes), and interior ceiling stains near exterior walls/top
  floor. Source: trade/UMN ice-dam guidance.

### 2.5 Aging, UV & thermal cycling

- **Granules** are the shingle's UV armor; sun/wind/rain loosen them over time
  and they wash into gutters. **Minor loss (10–20%)** adds only a year or two of
  aging; **bald patches (50%+ loss)** can cut remaining life by **up to ~70%**;
  loss exceeding **~30% of the surface** is a common rule-of-thumb for "beyond
  repair." Source: GAF (granule guidance); trade analyses (InterNACHI; roofing
  contractors).
- **Uniform, long-term granule loss = normal aging** (insurers usually treat it
  as wear, not storm damage); **localized loss in patterns / along impact paths
  = possible storm damage**. Source: InterNACHI "Mastering Roof Inspections."
- **ARMA:** proper maintenance can extend shingle lifespan by **~25–30%**.
  Source: ARMA (via trade summary — treat as approximate).
- Thermal cycling fatigues sealant bonds and fasteners; designers are advised to
  plan for **~100°F (≈56°C)** seasonal temperature swing across the roof.
  Source: Roofing Elements Magazine citing National Research Council of Canada.

### 2.6 Poor installation / workmanship

- IBHS identifies the two most common causes of high-wind shingle damage as
  **improper installation** and **using shingles not rated for the design wind
  speed**. Source: IBHS (via Florida resilient-roofing guidance).
- **Nailing:** ARMA specifies nails penetrate **≥ ¾ in (19 mm)** into solid deck
  (or **≥ ⅛ in** through thinner decks); **high-nailing** above the sealant line
  leaves the shingle's lower edge unsecured and is a frequent uplift/leak cause.
  Source: ARMA residential asphalt manual.
- **Shingle overhang at drip edge:** GAF says shingles should extend **⅜–¾ in
  (9–19 mm)** past the drip edge; **overhang > ¾ in is unsupported** and can
  crack off, reducing wind resistance. Drip edge should extend **≥ 2 in** onto
  the deck and **≥ ¼ in** below the sheathing edge, fastened **8–10 in on
  center**. Source: GAF; ARMA.
- **Ice-dam regions:** GAF says eave leak barrier should extend **≥ 24 in inside
  the interior wall line**; valleys protected with **36-in self-adhered
  underlayment (ASTM D1970)**. Source: GAF installation instructions.

### 2.7 Ponding / standing water (low-slope sections, porches, additions)

- **NRCA** defines ponding as **water remaining on the roof > 48 hours** after
  the rain stops (some guidance cites 24–48 hr; **48 hr is the primary
  threshold**). Source: NRCA; ARMA.
- **Why it harms the roof:** prolonged water chemically breaks down membranes and
  seam adhesives, leaches the oils out of asphalt (leaving it brittle/cracking),
  and magnifies UV. **Water weighs ~5 lb per inch per sq ft** — a 1-in pond over
  100 sq ft adds **~500 lb** of dead load, deflecting the deck and worsening the
  pond in a feedback loop. Source: NRCA; ARMA "Ponding Water Basics."
- **A "flat" roof still needs ≥ ¼ in/ft of slope** to drain. Common causes:
  improper slope, clogged drains/scuppers, sagging deck. Source: NRCA; IKO.

## 3. Warning signs — INTERIOR

| Sign | What it indicates | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Brown/yellow **ceiling or wall stains** (grow/darken after rain) | Active roof leak or trapped attic moisture; classic first sign | GAF; This Old House; trade |
| **Attic:** dark spots, damp/compressed insulation, mold on rafters, sagging sheathing | Leak or poor ventilation/condensation | GAF |
| **Daylight through the roof decking** (not through vents) | Holes in decking + shingles — usually means replacement, not patch | George J. Keller & Sons; This Old House |
| **Sagging ceiling or roofline** | Sheathing/deck decay from prolonged moisture — high-priority structural | GAF; trade |
| **Musty odor / mold growth** | Moisture intrusion (incl. ice-dam backup); health hazard | University of Minnesota; trade |
| **Peeling paint near roof overhangs** | Moisture wicking at eaves | This Old House |

> Distinguish a **roof leak** from **interior condensation** (poor attic
> ventilation) — they look similar on a ceiling but need different fixes.
> Source: GAF; Tadlock Roofing.

## 4. Warning signs — EXTERIOR

| Sign | What it indicates | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Missing / cracked / torn shingles** | Wind or impact damage; exposes underlayment & deck | GAF; trade |
| **Curling, cupping, buckling** | Aging, moisture, poor attic ventilation, or high-nailing | GAF; Colorado roofing guidance |
| **Blistering** ("bubbles") | Manufacturing defect or overheating from poor ventilation | InterNACHI |
| **Granule loss / bald or shiny patches** + **sandy grit in gutters/downspouts** | Shingles nearing end of life; accelerates once mat is exposed | GAF; InterNACHI; trade |
| **Rusted, lifted, bent, or missing flashing** at chimney/walls/skylights/valleys | The leading leak source (see §2.1) | This Old House; trade |
| **Exposed fasteners / gaps** at flashing | Install defect / wind lift | ASHI; trade |
| **Sagging gutters pulling from fascia** | Loose fasteners, debris weight, or rotted fascia behind | JG Roofing |
| **Dark streaks / algae** | Cosmetic-to-moisture issue; inspect underlying condition | This Old House |

## 5. Component-level failure points

| Component | Role | Primary failure mode | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Field shingles** | Shed water, UV barrier | Cracking, curling, granule loss, wind blow-off — but only ~5–10% of *leaks* | NRCA (via trade); InterNACHI |
| **Flashing** (step / counter / valley / chimney / drip edge) | Waterproof transitions & penetrations | Corrosion, short laps, lifted/bent, failed sealant; ~90–95% of leaks (trade estimate) | ARMA; ASHI; trade |
| **Underlayment / leak barrier** | Secondary water-shed; ice-dam & wind-rain backup | Becomes brittle with age; insufficient laps; omitted in valleys/eaves | NRCA; InterNACHI; GAF |
| **Pipe boots / vents** | Seal stack penetrations; attic airflow | Rubber cracks (10–15 yr life; 2–5 yr if nailed exposed) | Dom Roofing; Elite Roofing |
| **Fascia** | Edge board; carries gutters | Rot from gutter overflow; peeling paint, soft spots, sagging | Ledegar Roofing |
| **Soffit** | Eave underside; houses **intake** vents | Rot, blocked/painted-over vents reduce attic intake → heat & ice dams | Ledegar Roofing |
| **Gutters / downspouts** | Drain water from eaves | Sag, clog, overflow → fascia/soffit rot + ice dams; clean **2×/yr (spring+fall)** | GAF; JG Roofing |
| **Roof deck / sheathing** | Structural substrate | Decay from prolonged moisture → sagging, daylight holes | GAF; trade |
| **Attic ventilation** | Controls heat/moisture; extends shingle life; limits ice dams | Imbalanced/blocked vents | NRCA; ARMA; GAF |

**Ventilation numbers (for "proper ventilation" claims):** NRCA/ARMA recommend
**1 sq ft of net-free vent area per 150 sq ft of attic floor (1:150)**, or
**1:300** with a ceiling vapor retarder, **balanced ~50% intake (soffit) / 50%
exhaust (ridge)**. Source: NRCA; ARMA; GAF. (ARMA shortcut: attic sq ft ÷ 2 =
needed sq in of intake & of exhaust for 1:150; ÷ 4 for 1:300.)

## 7. Quick-reference numbers (all source-backed unless flagged)

- Shingle life: **3-tab 15–20 yr, architectural 25–30 yr** — NRCA/ARMA via Nat'l Roofing Authority
- Leaks at flashing: **~90–95%** (field shingles ~5–10%) — **trade estimate attributed to NRCA (secondary)**
- Severe wind: **≥ 58 mph (NOAA)**; 3-tab rated ~**60 mph**, architectural to **130 mph**
- Severe hail (warning): **≥ 0.75 in (NOAA)**; roof damage onset **~1.0 in** (3-tab), **~1.25 in** (most products), **2.0 in** damages all — AMS
- IBHS IR shingle test: **22 products / 5 makers**, **1.5 & 2.0 in** hailstones
- Roof-cover failure water entry: **~9 bathtubs per inch of rain**; sealing deck cuts intrusion **up to 95%** — **IBHS (directly verified)**
- Wind/hail = largest claim type: **~2.8%** of insured homes/yr (of ~5.6% with any claim, 2019–2023) — Triple-I
- Ponding: **> 48 hr** = defect (NRCA); water **~5 lb/in/sq ft**; flat roof needs **≥ ¼ in/ft** slope
- Granule loss: **10–20%** minor; **50%+** cuts life up to **~70%**; **>30%** ≈ beyond repair — GAF/trade
- Ventilation: **1:150** (or **1:300** w/ vapor retarder), **50/50 intake/exhaust** — NRCA/ARMA/GAF
- Pipe boot life: **10–15 yr** (2–5 yr if poorly installed)
- Gutter cleaning: **2×/yr (spring + fall)** — GAF
