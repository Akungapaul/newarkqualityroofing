## 1. NJ Uniform Construction Code (UCC) — Roofing Permit Rules

**Governing authority:** New Jersey Department of Community Affairs (NJ DCA), Division of Codes and Standards. The Uniform Construction Code is codified at **N.J.A.C. 5:23**.

### 1.1 The "ordinary maintenance" exemption (no permit) — N.J.A.C. 5:23-2.7

Under N.J.A.C. 5:23-2.7(a), *"Ordinary maintenance to structures may be made without filing a permit application with or giving notice to the construction official."*

**Roofing — detached one- and two-family dwellings (current code text, N.J.A.C. 5:23-2.7(c)1.x):**

> *"The repair or replacement of existing roof covering on detached one- and two-family dwellings."*
> — Source: N.J.A.C. 5:23-2.7, official NJ DCA regulation PDF (nj.gov/dca/codes/codreg)

The NJ DCA's March 5, 2018 alert "Significant Changes to Minor Work and Ordinary Maintenance" states this as:

> *"Roofing – The repair or replacement (including total replacement) of any amount of existing roof covering on detached one- or two-family dwellings."*
> — Source: NJ DCA, "Significant Changes to Minor Work and Ordinary Maintenance," March 5, 2018 (nj.gov/dca/codes/alerts)

**Plain meaning:** For a detached 1- or 2-family home, a **complete re-roof / tear-off and replacement of the roof covering** (shingles, underlayment, flashing) is **ordinary maintenance and requires NO construction permit**, no inspection, and no notice to the construction official. This is the practical rule for most NQR residential repair/reroof jobs in Newark.

### 1.2 The 25% rule — all OTHER structures

For structures **other than** detached 1- and 2-family dwellings (e.g., commercial buildings, townhouses, condos, attached/multi-family), ordinary maintenance for roofing is limited. The legacy/general provision (N.J.A.C. 5:23-2.7(c)) for roofing is:

> *"The repair of existing roofing material not exceeding 25 percent of the total roof area within any 12 month period."*
> — Source: N.J.A.C. 5:23-2.7(c), ordinary maintenance listing

**Plain meaning:** On commercial, multi-family, or attached dwellings, repairing **more than 25% of the total roof area in a 12-month period** is no longer ordinary maintenance and **requires a permit**.

### 1.3 What ALWAYS requires a permit (structural exclusions) — N.J.A.C. 5:23-2.7(b)

Ordinary maintenance **never** includes structural work. Per 5:23-2.7(b), ordinary maintenance shall NOT include *"the cutting away of any wall, partition or portion thereof,"* the removal/cutting of any structural beam or load-bearing support, or any work affecting structural or fire safety.
— Source: N.J.A.C. 5:23-2.7(b)

**Therefore a permit IS required when a roofing job involves:**
- Replacing or altering **rafters, trusses, ridge beams, or other load-bearing roof structural members**.
- New roof **decking/sheathing as a structural change** or any change to the roof structure (the ordinary-maintenance roofing items cover roof *covering*, not structural framing). **[UNVERIFIED — exact treatment of sheathing-only replacement]** The code's ordinary-maintenance roofing item is worded for "roof covering"; replacement of deteriorated sheathing as part of like-for-like recovering is commonly treated as part of the covering work, but localities vary — confirm with the Newark Division of Buildings.
- Roof work on **commercial, townhouse, condo, or other attached/multi-family** buildings exceeding the 25% threshold.
- A **structural change** to the roof (changing pitch, adding dormers, framing changes).

### 1.4 Rehabilitation Subcode — roof recover vs. tear-off rules (N.J.A.C. 5:23-6.4)

When a permit IS triggered (commercial, structural, etc.), the Rehabilitation Subcode requires **complete removal of the existing roof covering** (no recover-over) when:
1. The existing roof/roof covering is **water-soaked or deteriorated** so it is not adequate as a base for additional roofing;
2. The existing covering is **wood shake, slate, clay, cement, or asbestos-cement tile**; or
3. **Two or more applications** of any roof covering already exist (i.e., no third layer).
— Source: N.J.A.C. 5:23-6.4 (Rehabilitation Subcode — Repairs), via Cornell LII / NJ DCA

### 1.5 Inspections

For ordinary-maintenance re-roofs on 1-2 family homes, **no inspection is required** (the exemption removes permit, inspection, and notice). When a permit is required (commercial/structural), standard UCC inspection requirements under N.J.A.C. 5:23 apply, including required inspections at the prescribed stages for the building subcode work.
— Source: N.J.A.C. 5:23-2.7; general UCC inspection framework N.J.A.C. 5:23-2.18 **[UNVERIFIED — specific roof inspection hold-points not separately confirmed in this pass]**

### 1.6 Newark / Essex County specifics

Permit administration is handled by each municipality's construction code enforcement agency. In Newark this is the **City of Newark Department of Economic & Housing Development, Division of Buildings / construction office**. The state UCC rules above apply uniformly; municipalities **enforce** but do not override the state ordinary-maintenance classification.
— Source: N.J.A.C. 5:23 (statewide UCC), NJ DCA. **[UNVERIFIED — no Newark-specific ordinance adding requirements beyond the state UCC was located in this pass; municipal interpretation of borderline cases can vary, so verify with Newark's construction office.]**

## 2. NJ Home Improvement Contractor (HIC) Registration — NJ Division of Consumer Affairs

**Governing law:** The **Contractors' Registration Act, N.J.S.A. 56:8-136 et seq.** (part of the Consumer Fraud Act). Administered by the **NJ Division of Consumer Affairs, Office of Consumer Protection, Regulated Business Section** — mailing address 124 Halsey Street, 7th Floor, P.O. Box 46016, **Newark, NJ 07101**.
— Source: NJ Division of Consumer Affairs, "Instructions for Registering as a Home Improvement Contractor."

### 2.1 Who must register

> *"Every business engaging in the business of making or selling home improvements, whether an individually owned business or a corporation, limited liability company, partnership, or other business entity, is required to register annually with the New Jersey Division of Consumer Affairs … unless specifically exempted from registration under the Act."*
> — Source: NJ DCA, HIC Registration Instructions

### 2.2 Registration number format

Each registered HIC receives a unique registration number in the format **"13VH" + digits** (a 13-character "13VH########" number). The statute (N.J.S.A. 56:8-144) requires it be **prominently displayed**:

> *"All registrants shall prominently display their registration numbers within their places of business, in all advertisements distributed within this State, on business documents, contracts and correspondence with consumers of home improvement services in this State, and on all commercial vehicles registered in this State and leased or owned by registrants and used by registrants for the purpose of providing home improvements."*
> — Source: N.J.S.A. 56:8-144, "Display of registration number; requirements."

The "13VH" prefix is the standard NJ HIC number format issued by the Division. **[UNVERIFIED — exact total digit count]** Public sources describe it as a 13-character number beginning with 13VH; verify NQR's own number from the Consumer Affairs verification portal (njconsumeraffairs.gov verify-a-contractor).

### 2.3 Insurance requirement (commercial general liability)

> *"On or after December 31, 2005, every registered contractor who is engaged in home improvements shall secure, maintain and file with the director proof of a certificate of commercial general liability insurance in a minimum amount of $500,000 per occurrence."*
> — Source: N.J.S.A. 56:8-142, "Proof of commercial general liability insurance; requirements." Confirmed in HIC Registration Instructions: *"An insurance certificate confirming that you are the named insured under a policy of commercial general liability insurance in a minimum amount of $500,000 per occurrence."*

**Workers' compensation:** The application requires a certificate of workers' compensation insurance unless legally exempt.
— Source: NJ DCA HIC application materials. **[UNVERIFIED as an explicit statutory minimum — WC is required by NJ employment law, attached to the HIC application, but is not a dollar-amount minimum in the Registration Act itself.]**

### 2.4 Fees

The NJ DCA initial HIC registration instructions packet (Rev. 2/1/05) lists an application fee of **$90** (non-refundable). Current 2026 published guidance commonly cites **$110 initial / $90 renewal**, with registration now on a periodic renewal cycle.
— Source: NJ DCA HIC Registration Instructions (original $90); secondary 2025–2026 guidance ($110/$90). **[UNVERIFIED — current exact fee should be confirmed on njconsumeraffairs.gov, as fee amounts and the registration cycle (annual vs. 2-year) have changed over time.]**

### 2.5 Contract / advertising requirements (consumer-facing)

The Act (N.J.S.A. 56:8-151 region) requires home improvement contracts over a threshold amount to be in writing and to include the contractor's **legal name, business address, and registration number**, plus a **copy of the certificate of commercial general liability insurance and the insurer's telephone number**, and the total price.
— Source: N.J.S.A. 56:8 (Contractors' Registration Act / regulations N.J.A.C. 13:45A-16/17).

**Advertising trigger:** Anyone who advertises as a contractor for covered home improvements in NJ is subject to the Act (N.J.S.A. 56:8-139). Simple residential alphabetical phone listings are excepted.
— Source: N.J.S.A. 56:8-139.

## 3. NJ Climate Stressors on Roofs (Newark / Essex County focus)

**Reference station:** Newark Liberty International Airport (EWR), GHCND:USW00014734 — NOAA 1991–2020 U.S. Climate Normals.

### 3.1 Snowfall & snow load

- **Average annual snowfall, Newark (EWR):** **~31.5 inches** (80 cm) — NOAA 1991–2020 normals.
  - Source: NOAA 1991–2020 Climate Normals (Newark Liberty Intl), via Current Results / Wikipedia tabulation.
  - Snowiest months: **February ~10.1 in, January ~9.1 in, December ~5.4 in** — ~78% of annual snow falls Dec–Feb.
- **Snow season:** Most NJ snowfall occurs **mid-November through mid-April**.
  - Source: Wikipedia "Climate of New Jersey" (NOAA-sourced).
- **Ground snow load (roof design):** Northern/central NJ ground snow load **Pg ≈ 25 psf** under ASCE 7-16 (used in the NJ UCC building subcode); higher-elevation NW counties (Sussex, Warren, Morris) run **25–30 psf**.
  - Source: ASCE 7-16 ground snow load maps; NJ UCC Building Subcode (N.J.A.C. 5:23 adopting IBC/IRC). **[UNVERIFIED — exact Essex County Pg value; ASCE 7-22 revised NJ snow loads, so confirm the value for the specific jurisdiction/code edition in force.]**
- **Snow weight rule of thumb:** Fresh snow ≈ 3–5 lb/ft² per foot of depth; wet/heavy snow can exceed **20 lb/ft²**.
  - Source: industry guidance (Elegant Exteriors / Proven Contracting). **[UNVERIFIED as a code value — these are general physical estimates, not the design load.]**

### 3.2 Freeze-thaw cycles

- Newark winters cross the 32°F freezing point frequently. Average **January low ≈ 25.5°F**, average July high ≈ 86.9°F (EWR, 1991–2020), so daily temps repeatedly swing across freezing in winter, driving freeze-thaw stress on flashing, sealants, fasteners, and any trapped moisture (water expands ~9% on freezing).
  - Source: NOAA 1991–2020 Normals (EWR temperatures); physics of ice expansion (general).
- **Freeze-thaw cycle count:** Industry/regional estimates put a typical north-NJ winter at roughly **35–45 freeze-thaw cycles**.
  - Source: regional roofing guidance. **[UNVERIFIED — not a NOAA-published metric; treat the 35–45 figure as an estimate, not an official statistic.]**

### 3.3 Nor'easters & coastal storms

- **Frequency:** NOAA's New Jersey State Climate Summary: *"Annually, the state experiences at least one coastal storm, but some years have seen as many as 5 to 10 storm events."* Damaging nor'easters are most common **October–April**.
  - Source: NOAA / NCICS, "New Jersey State Climate Summary 2022" (statesummaries.ncics.org/chapter/nj).
- **Impact context:** Superstorm Sandy (2012) drove ~$29.4B in NJ damages with 9–10 ft storm surge — illustrates the high-wind/water load nor'easters and tropical systems can impose.
  - Source: NOAA NJ State Climate Summary.

### 3.4 Wind

- **Design wind speed:** Mid-Atlantic / northern NJ basic (ultimate) design wind speed under ASCE 7-16 is roughly **110–115 mph** for typical risk-category-II buildings.
  - Source: ASCE 7-16 wind speed maps (windload.solutions tabulation); NJ UCC. **[UNVERIFIED — exact Essex County value/map zone; confirm against the code edition adopted in NJ.]**
- Nor'easters and winter storms commonly bring **40–60 mph sustained winds with gusts to 70+ mph** in north NJ. **[UNVERIFIED — gust figures are NWS event-level, not a normal/annual statistic.]**
- Coastal NJ adds **salt-air corrosion** stress on fasteners and metal components (less acute inland in Essex County but relevant for fasteners/flashing).
  - Source: regional roofing guidance. **[UNVERIFIED as a quantified stat.]**

### 3.5 Hail & thunderstorms

- NJ averages roughly **25–30 thunderstorms per year**.
  - Source: Wikipedia "Climate of New Jersey."
- **Hail frequency, Essex County:** No authoritative IBHS/NOAA per-county hail-frequency figure was confirmed in this pass. Essex County does experience hail with summer thunderstorms, but it is **not** a high-frequency hail region like the Plains/"hail alley."
  - Source: IBHS hail risk-research program (ibhs.org) describes hail mitigation generally; NOAA SPC is the authoritative hail-event database. **[UNVERIFIED — specific Essex County hail-days/year not sourced. Class 4 impact-rated shingles are the standard hail mitigation per IBHS FORTIFIED guidance.]**

### 3.6 Temperature trend (longevity context)

- NJ temperatures have risen **>3.5°F since the early 20th century**; extreme precipitation events have increased.
  - Source: NOAA / NCICS NJ State Climate Summary 2022.
