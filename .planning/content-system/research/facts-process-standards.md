## 1. Standard Roof Repair Workflow

The industry-typical repair sequence is **inspection → diagnosis → documentation
→ stabilization → repair → verification → cleanup → warranty/follow-up**. The
discrete steps below are synthesized from contractor process descriptions; they
are an industry-common pattern, not a single codified standard.

| # | Step | What happens | Source / status |
|---|------|--------------|-----------------|
| 1 | **Inspection** | Licensed contractor assesses the system for wear, damage, structural weakness. Often starts on the ground and in the attic to trace water paths, then checks shingles, flashing (vents/chimneys), valleys, and penetrations (skylights, pipes). May use binoculars, drone, or infrared moisture/heat imaging. | Integrity Home Exteriors; North Coast Roofing |
| 2 | **Diagnosis** | Determine root cause vs. symptom (e.g., trace an interior stain back to failed flashing, not just the visible drip point). | Integrity Home Exteriors |
| 3 | **Documentation** | Photograph damaged areas ("see what they see"); deliver a written report with scope, recommended repairs, and a cost estimate covering labor, materials, timeline, contingencies, and warranty. | Integrity Home Exteriors; North Coast Roofing |
| 4 | **Stabilization** | If water is actively entering, tarp or temporary patch FIRST to stop the leak; schedule permanent repair once materials arrive and weather allows. | Integrity Home Exteriors |
| 5 | **Surface prep** | Clean the work area for proper adhesion — debris removal, membrane cleaning, drying before patching. | Integrity Home Exteriors |
| 6 | **Repair execution** | Protect the zone with tarps; replace damaged shingles, reseal flashing, verify underlayment tie-in; match color/style to existing materials. | Integrity Home Exteriors |
| 7 | **Verification** | Post-work inspection confirms correct execution; review surrounding areas for incidental damage. | Integrity Home Exteriors |
| 8 | **Cleanup** | Remove all debris and leftover materials; magnet-sweep/collect nails and fasteners around the home. | Integrity Home Exteriors |
| 9 | **Final walkthrough + warranty** | Walk the homeowner through the work with stage photos; note what to watch after the next rain; provide workmanship warranty. | Integrity Home Exteriors |

## 2. Inspection Standards & Frequency

### Inspection frequency (NRCA)
- NRCA recommends roof system inspections **at least twice per year — spring and
  fall**. Source: NRCA / GSM Roofing summary of NRCA preventive-maintenance guidance.
- NRCA also recommends an **additional inspection after any major weather event**
  (large storm, hurricane, hail, heavy snow load, severe wind, earthquake).
  Source: NRCA guidance; Roof It Forward summary of NRCA + HBA recommendation.
- Rationale: spring inspection follows winter stress; fall inspection precedes it.

### Inspection / material standards (ASTM, ARMA, NRCA)
- **ASTM D3462 / D3462M** — Standard Specification for Asphalt Shingles Made from
  Glass Felt and Surfaced with Mineral Granules. Covers materials & manufacture,
  physical requirements (pliability, tear resistance, nail pull-through),
  dimensions/masses, workmanship/finish/appearance, sampling, test methods,
  inspection, and rejection. Source: ASTM (store.astm.org D3462/D3462M-19); ARMA.
- **Critical scope limit (cite this carefully):** ASTM D3462 evaluates product
  **as manufactured**. ARMA states testing for D3462 compliance "**after
  application and during the service life of the shingles is beyond the scope
  of, and therefore not permitted by, the standard.**" Compliance is measured at
  manufacture; testing may occur by buyer–seller agreement after manufacture but
  **before installation**. Source: ARMA, "When Does a Shingle Comply with ASTM
  D3462?"; corroborated by NRCA.
- **NRCA position:** NRCA *recommends* shingles that comply with ASTM D3462 and
  notes not all asphalt shingles comply. Deeper guidance lives in *The NRCA Steep
  Roofing Manual* and *The NRCA Roofing and Waterproofing Manual*. Source: NRCA.

### Wind-resistance standards (for "storm-rated"/"wind-rated" claims)
- **ASTM D3161** (physical lab test, fan-induced): shingles tested 2 hours at a
  set velocity. Class A = 60 mph, Class D = 90 mph, **Class F = 110 mph**.
  Source: AIC Roofing; DML USA summarizing ASTM D3161.
- **ASTM D7158** (uplift calculation method): Class D = 90 mph, **Class G =
  120 mph, Class H = 150 mph**. Source: AIC Roofing summarizing ASTM D7158.
- Building-code note: shingles classified **ASTM D3161 Class F, TAS 107, or ASTM
  D7158 Class H** are acceptable in all wind zones. Source: AIC Roofing.
- ⚠️ Use only if NQR actually installs/verifies a given wind class. Stating a
  wind class NQR doesn't verify on a specific product is **[UNVERIFIED]**.

## 3. Warranty Norms (Workmanship vs. Material)

### Two distinct warranties
- **Workmanship (labor) warranty** — issued by the **contractor**; covers install
  errors: leaks from improper installation, loose field shingles, poorly sealed
  flashing, tear-off/install mistakes. Source: Cobex; Owens Corning blog.
- **Manufacturer material warranty** — issued by the **manufacturer**; covers
  defective materials / factory defects (shingles, underlayment). Source: same.

### Typical durations (industry ranges — cite as "typical," not as NQR's terms)
- **Workmanship:** standard terms run **1–5 years**; reputable contractors offer
  **10+ years**; manufacturer-backed certified programs extend workmanship to
  **up to 25 years**. Source: Cobex; Indy Roof & Restoration.
- **Material:** typically **20–50 years**; many architectural asphalt shingles
  carry a **limited lifetime** term with a non-prorated "protection" window
  **commonly 10–15 years**, after which coverage becomes **prorated**. Source:
  RoofSmart; Roof-Crafters.

> NQR's own workmanship warranty length is **[UNVERIFIED]** — get the real number
> from the client. Do not publish a specific NQR warranty term without it.

## 4. Manufacturer Contractor Certifications

All three are top-tier, restricted designations that **unlock the manufacturer's
best system warranties** (50-yr material + 25-yr workmanship), which standard
contractors cannot offer.

### GAF — Master Elite®
| Requirement | Detail | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Experience | **Minimum 7 years** in the roofing business | AKVM; Johnson Restoration; corroborated across sources |
| Licensing | Properly licensed in state/province | GAF program guidelines; AKVM |
| Insurance | **≥ $1M** general liability | AKVM; Roofon-tier sources |
| Reputation | Satisfactory BBB rating, positive reviews, good credit rating | AKVM |
| Exclusivity | Often cited as **top ~2%** of contractors | AKVM (note: "top 2%" is a marketing-common figure; treat as approximate) |
| Renewal | Certification **valid 1 year**, must re-qualify | AKVM |
| **Warranty unlocked** | **GAF Golden Pledge®**: **50-yr material (non-prorated), 25-yr workmanship**, plus tear-off & disposal. Requires a GAF shingle + **5 qualifying GAF accessories**. | Roof-Crafters; Gunner Roofing |

## 5. Quick-Reference Numbers (all source-backed)

- Inspection cadence: **2×/year (spring + fall)** + after major weather — NRCA
- Wind: D3161 **Class F = 110 mph**; D7158 **Class G = 120 / Class H = 150 mph**
- Workmanship warranty typical: **1–5 yrs standard, 10–25 yrs certified**
- Material warranty typical: **20–50 yrs**, lifetime non-prorated window **10–15 yrs**
- GAF Master Elite: **7 yrs exp, $1M liability**, Golden Pledge **50/25**
- OC Platinum Preferred: **invite-only, ~top 1%, 3 yrs, $1M**, Platinum **50/25**
- CertainTeed SSM: **5 yrs exp, 50% crew MSA**, 5-STAR **50/25**, register in **60 days**
