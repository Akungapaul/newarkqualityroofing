# Caldwells–Roseland Essex County Cities — Fact Bank (Cities Batch D)

House style = **name-only attribution** in prose (cite the body by name; no outbound
links / URLs in on-page copy). The five cities here share one NJ UCC permit rule and one
climate baseline — do NOT re-derive either; cross-reference the shared packs. What
differs city-to-city is the **construction office**, the **historic COA gate** (which
varies sharply — read Rule 1 carefully; this batch spans the full spectrum from
advisory-only to a real ordinance-with-COA), the **Census housing profile**, the
**verified neighborhoods**, and the **geography (upland vs. floodplain)**.

These are the **caldwells-roseland archetype**: far-western Essex County boroughs and
townships, four of them affluent upland communities ("the Caldwells," Essex Fells,
Roseland) with a shared **mature street-tree canopy** roofing stressor framed
QUALITATIVELY (leaf/debris clogging valleys and gutters, branch-impact in nor'easters
and summer storms, shade-driven moss/algae on north-facing slopes). The exceptions split
two ways: **Caldwell** is the renter-heavier, walkable downtown borough (41.0%
owner-occupied — majority renter — with Caldwell University and the Bloomfield Avenue
corridor); **Fairfield** is the low-lying Passaic-River **floodplain** township bisected
by the Route 46 / I-80 commercial-industrial corridor (a large flat/low-slope commercial
roof market), NOT an upland community. Only **North Caldwell** of the five touches a
county reservation (Hilltop). Only **Fairfield** and **Roseland's western edge** carry
documentable Passaic-River floodplain exposure — never attach floodplain framing to
Caldwell, North Caldwell, or Essex Fells.

### 1. HISTORIC COA GATE per city (CRITICAL — the binding gate is the LOCAL ordinance + COA from the municipal HPC, NOT Register listing, NOT a state-owned site, NOT a museum)

**The binding historic gate is the LOCAL ordinance + COA from the municipal HPC, NOT
National/State Register listing, NOT a state-owned historic site, and NOT a
historical-society museum.** Per the National Park Service, "Listing in the National
Register places no federal restrictions on a private property owner" — Register listing
alone never restricts a private reroof. A state-owned site (Grover Cleveland Birthplace),
a federal park, and a society-owned museum (Williams-Harrison House) are likewise NOT
municipal COA gates and impose no rule on a neighbor's roof. Only assert a COA
requirement where a LOCAL designation is CONFIRMED, and only for a parcel that IS on the
local designation list / inside a designated district. This batch spans the full
spectrum — do NOT flatten it.

- **Caldwell = YES, but ONLY for the two individually LOCALLY DESIGNATED landmarks (NO
  designated district).** The Borough of Caldwell HAS a real local Historic Preservation
  Commission (HPC) and ordinance — codified as **Chapter 130 "Historic Preservation"
  (§130-1 to §130-13)** of the Caldwell Borough Code (Ord. 1313-15, adopted 11-17-2015).
  Per Preservation New Jersey, Caldwell has **TWO locally designated individual
  LANDMARKS — one being the Caldwell Public Library** (a 1917 Classical Revival Carnegie
  library; local landmark since 2016; the SECOND site is UNNAMED by any source — do NOT
  name it). For a parcel that IS one of those designated landmarks, exterior roofing work
  routes through the HPC's Certificate-of-Appropriateness review before a permit. **BUT
  Caldwell has NOT adopted any local historic DISTRICT** — the NJ Historic Trust calls
  them "at least three POTENTIAL districts" identified by a 2017 architectural survey, NOT
  enacted. Correct framing: "Caldwell has a local Historic Preservation Commission and
  ordinance (Chapter 130); if your property is one of the borough's two locally
  designated historic landmarks, exterior roofing routes through the HPC's
  Certificate-of-Appropriateness review before a permit — but Caldwell has not designated
  a local historic district, so a typical home is not in a COA-regulated district."
  CRITIC CORRECTION: the North Caldwell research's claim that "Caldwell has a designated
  downtown historic-district overlay and a COA process" is **WRONG** — that overlay
  language (HD-1 / HD-2 / HD-3) belongs to **Caldwell, IDAHO**, not Caldwell, NJ. Do NOT
  reintroduce it.
  - **Grover Cleveland Birthplace = STATE-owned site, NOT a homeowner COA gate.** The
    Grover Cleveland Birthplace (Caldwell Presbyterian Church Manse), 207 Bloomfield
    Avenue, is Caldwell's ONLY National/State Register property. It is STATE-OWNED
    (administered by the NJ Division of Parks & Forestry with the Grover Cleveland
    Birthplace Memorial Association). Per NPS, Register listing places no restriction on a
    private owner, and a state-owned museum is not a reroof gate. Heritage color ONLY.
  - **Caldwell Public Library status is IN FLUX** — it is a LOCAL landmark (NOT NRHP;
    only the Birthplace is on the Register), but it appeared on Preservation NJ's 2019/2022
    "10 Most Endangered" and is under active demolition/redevelopment planning (borough
    Municipal Complex plan). Use it as the named local-landmark example but do NOT present
    it as a stable, visitable COA anchor; the live status is changing.
- **North Caldwell = NO binding COA (HPC is ADVISORY/SURVEY-ONLY).** The Borough of North
  Caldwell HAS a local Historic Preservation Commission established by ordinance —
  **Chapter 107 (Zoning and Land Use), Article XIII "Historic Preservation Commission,"
  §§ 107-85 to 107-87** (authorized by N.J.S.A. 40:55D-107). BUT under § 107-87 the
  Commission is **ADVISORY/SURVEY-ONLY** — it prepares a survey, makes recommendations,
  and advises boards; it issues **NO Certificate of Appropriateness** and has designated
  **NO local district or landmark.** Therefore NO COA applies to a homeowner's reroof
  anywhere in North Caldwell. A revised HPC ordinance that WOULD add COA review
  (**O-8-2026**) was only INTRODUCED, not adopted, and designates nothing — do NOT cite it
  as in force. NO North Caldwell property is on the National or NJ State Register. Correct
  framing: "North Caldwell has an advisory Historic Preservation Commission, but with no
  locally designated districts or landmarks, no Certificate of Appropriateness applies to
  a homeowner's reroof."
- **Essex Fells = NO — no local HPC, no ordinance, no COA, no Register listing.** Confirmed
  via the borough's eCode360 code (ES2826) and Building Department page: Essex Fells has
  **NO local historic-preservation ordinance, NO Historic Preservation Commission, and NO
  Certificate-of-Appropriateness process.** A homeowner reroof requires NO historic-board
  approval. There is **NO "Essex Fells Historic District" on the National or NJ Register**
  (the brief's assumption that one "likely" exists is REFUTED — the Essex County NRHP list
  and NJ DEP HPO Essex County list contain no Essex Fells entry). The borough's
  planned-community (Bowditch, ~1889–1902) heritage is real COLOR but carries no Register
  listing and no private-owner restriction. Do NOT confuse the code's Ch. 142 "HISTORIC
  STRUCTURE" wording — that is the FEMA/NFIP floodplain-management definition, NOT a
  preservation designation. The 2018 Master Plan "Historic Preservation Element" is
  conditional/aspirational ("could be the basis for..."), not enacted law.
- **Fairfield = NO binding COA (HPC is ADVISORY/EDUCATIONAL only).** Fairfield Township's
  code DOES list a Historic Preservation Commission, but its powers are
  **ADVISORY/EDUCATIONAL/CELEBRATORY** plus recommending programs for the township-owned
  Peter Van Ness House — it issues **NO binding Certificate of Appropriateness** and there
  is **NO locally designated historic district.** A homeowner reroof requires NO historic
  approval. Correct framing: "Fairfield has an advisory Historic Preservation Commission
  focused on education and the township-owned Van Ness House; it does not require a
  Certificate of Appropriateness for private exterior/roofing work." Do NOT publish a
  precise HPC ordinance section number (the "§2-55"-style cite surfaced only in a
  secondary AI summary and was not verified — cite qualitatively).
  - **Van Ness House (236 Little Falls Rd) and Fairfield Dutch Reformed Church (Fairfield
    Rd) = REGISTER-listed, township/church-owned — NOT homeowner COA gates.** The Van Ness
    House is NRHP-listed (7-29-1977) AND township-owned (named to Preservation NJ's 2019
    "10 Most Endangered"); the Dutch Reformed Church is NRHP-listed (1975) and
    church-owned. Per NPS, Register listing alone places no restriction on a private owner.
    Heritage color ONLY. Do NOT attribute the Israel Crane House to Fairfield — that is in
    MONTCLAIR.
- **Roseland = a real ordinance + COA process EXISTS, but NO property is confirmed locally
  DESIGNATED — so NO homeowner is subject to a COA absent a designation.** Roseland HAS
  the **Roseland Landmarks and Historic District Commission**, codified at **Chapter 30
  (Land Development), Article IX "Historic Preservation," §§ 30-901 through 30-910** (the
  COA process for "major" alterations sits at § 30-909). This is a real on-the-books
  ordinance, NOT merely advisory. **BUT no source confirms any specific local landmark,
  landmark site, or historic district has actually been DESIGNATED**, and § 30-901.1
  requires **OWNER CONSENT** before any residence can be designated. The COA gate applies
  ONLY to locally designated properties — and none are confirmed. Correct framing:
  "Roseland maintains a Landmarks and Historic District Commission and a Certificate of
  Appropriateness process under Chapter 30, Article IX, but the binding COA gate applies
  only to LOCALLY DESIGNATED properties; no homeowner is subject to it unless their
  property has been locally designated."
  - **Williams-Harrison House (a/k/a Harrison House, 126 Eagle Rock Ave) = Register-listed
    + historical-society museum — NOT a homeowner COA gate.** Built 1824 (Federal),
    NJ Register (11-24-1978) + NRHP (3-13-1979), owned/operated as a museum by the Roseland
    Historical Society. No evidence of LOCAL designation. Per NPS, Register listing imposes
    no restriction on private owners. Heritage color ONLY; never a township-wide or
    neighbor COA.

### 2. CONSTRUCTION / PERMIT OFFICE per city

- **All five** apply the same statewide UCC rule (cross-ref
  facts-nj-regulatory-climate.md §1): a **detached one- or two-family dwelling reroof —
  including a full tear-off and replacement of the roof covering — is "ordinary
  maintenance" under N.J.A.C. 5:23-2.7 and requires NO construction permit, no inspection,
  and no notice.** A permit IS required on commercial, multi-family, or attached buildings
  (the 25% rule) and for any structural roof work; recover-vs-tear-off is governed by
  Rehab Subcode 5:23-6.4. Each city enforces but does not override the state
  classification. A COA on a locally designated property is a **SEPARATE** approval — not a
  substitute for, nor a trigger of, a building permit; a 1–2 family reroof stays UCC
  "ordinary maintenance" even where a COA is required.

### 4. PASSAIC-RIVER FLOODPLAIN — Fairfield (and Roseland's western edge) ONLY; a roof-relevant DRAINAGE/storm stressor

- **Fairfield is the defining floodplain city.** Fairfield sits in the PASSAIC RIVER
  floodplain in the NW corner of Essex County, downstream of the Passaic-Pompton
  confluence ("Two Bridges"). It maintains a Special Flood Hazard Area / 100-year flood
  zone map and FEMA inundation maps for the Pine Brook and Singac areas, and points
  residents to NJ's Mitigation Assistance Program and the Blue Acres buyout program.
  **Great Piece Meadows** (a Passaic-River freshwater wetland, ~1,170 acres of it within
  Fairfield per Wikipedia) lies partly in the township; the adjacent **Hatfield Swamp /
  West Essex Park** wetlands form the same Passaic system bordering Fairfield. The
  **NOAA-NWS Passaic River at Pine Brook gauge (PINN4)** is used to judge Fairfield flood
  severity. Named major floods: **Hurricane Irene (Aug 2011)**, **remnants of Hurricane
  Ida (Sept 2021, neighborhood evacuation + state of emergency)**, and **Hurricane Floyd
  (Sept 1999)**. Frame the floodplain as a roof-relevant **DRAINAGE/storm** stressor
  (positive low-slope drainage, sound flashing, well-maintained gutters/scuppers/
  downspouts) — NOT a basement-flood sales claim. CUTS: do NOT publish the unsourced
  "~70% of the town is in a flood zone" figure (secondary moving-guide only), do NOT
  publish specific PINN4 flood-stage numbers in feet (unverified), and drop/hedge the
  total Great Piece Meadows complex acreage (sources disagree ~4,100 vs ~7,100 ac — use
  the ~1,170-ac in-township figure).
- **Roseland's WESTERN edge carries localized floodplain only.** Roseland's western
  municipal boundary IS the Passaic River (border with East Hanover, Morris County). Per
  Roseland's own Master Plan, ~30% (724 acres) of the borough is environmentally
  constrained, including **459 acres within the FEMA Special Flood Hazard Area** and 574
  acres of wetlands; part of **West Essex Park** occupies the western edge, and Roseland
  is an NFIP participant with a Flood Damage Prevention ordinance (Chapter 22). Frame this
  as a roof-relevant DRAINAGE/storm stressor on the WESTERN/RIVERINE side — NOT a
  whole-borough flood claim (the office corridors and most residential neighborhoods sit
  on higher developed ground).
- **Caldwell, North Caldwell, and Essex Fells are UPLAND — NO floodplain framing.** Do
  NOT attach Passaic floodplain / Hatfield Swamp / FEMA-flood-zone framing to any of these
  three. North Caldwell does NOT border the Passaic River (that is West Caldwell's western
  border); the brief's "Passaic forms North Caldwell's northern boundary" is
  unconfirmed/contradicted — OMIT it.
