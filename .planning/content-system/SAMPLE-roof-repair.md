## How Do You Know If You Need Roof Repair?

**6 warning signs indicate a roof needs repair: ceiling or wall stains, missing or cracked shingles, granule loss in gutters, lifted or rusted flashing, daylight through the roof deck, and a sagging roofline.**

6 warning signs separate a repairable roof from a failing one:

- **Brown or yellow ceiling and wall stains** that spread or darken after rain indicate an active roof leak or trapped attic moisture, per GAF and This Old House.
- **Missing, cracked, or torn shingles** expose the underlayment and the roof deck to wind-driven rain, per GAF inspection guidance.
- **Granule loss with sandy grit in gutters** indicates shingles nearing end of life; granule loss exceeding 30% of the surface is the common rule-of-thumb for beyond repair, per GAF.
- **Rusted, lifted, or bent flashing** at chimneys, walls, skylights, and valleys is the most common leak source, because flashing seals the roof transitions that 90–95% of leaks trace back to (industry estimate attributed to the NRCA).
- **Daylight through the roof deck** seen from inside the attic indicates holes in the decking and shingles — a sign that points toward replacement rather than a patch, per This Old House.
- **A sagging ceiling or roofline** indicates sheathing decay from prolonged moisture and ranks as a structural priority, per GAF.

These 6 warning signs set the scope of a Newark roof-repair assessment; a roof showing heavy wear before the 15–30-year asphalt-shingle service window points to premature failure — an install, ventilation, or storm event — not age alone, per NRCA and ARMA service-life guidance.

## When Does Roof Repair Beat Roof Replacement?

**Roof repair beats roof replacement when damage covers under 25–30% of the roof area and the asphalt roof sits under 10–15 years of age**, per the contractor-consensus repair-vs-replace thresholds. 3 decision rules separate a repair from a replacement:

- **Repair the roof, when damage covers under 25–30% of the roof area** — the contractor-consensus "25–30% rule" that points beyond that share toward replacement.
- **Replace the roof, when one repair approaches 50% of replacement cost** — the contractor-consensus "50% rule" that favors replacement over a high-cost patch.
- **Repair the roof, when the asphalt roof sits under 10–15 years of age and the damage stays localized**, because asphalt shingles carry a 15–30-year service life, per NRCA and ARMA service-life guidance.

Daylight through the roof deck or a sagging roofline overrides the cost rules and points to replacement, per This Old House and GAF. ([Full repair-vs-replace cost ratios and the 50%/30% rule math live in the linked comparison page, **Roof Repair vs Roof Replacement**.])
